S PRESIDENCY OF THE COUNCIL OF MINISTERS

tividades The transposition into the digital environment of essential functions of institutional

do em harmonia strengthening of the regulatory and organisational framework for cybersecurity,
carried out in harmony with the whole area and in defence against common cyber

gravidade This legislative initiative arises from the awareness not only of the pressing
o potencial seriousness posed by multiple cyber threats, but also of the high disruptive
rioso um reforço potential of their hostile actions against digital assets, making it imperative to
dicionar a strengthen national capacity for the prevention of acts that may compromise
s funcionais e national security and interest, as well as the multiple functional and productive
dynamics of Portuguese society.
Indeed, in view of the notable increase in the quantity and sophistication of threats,
as well as the growing use of, and dependence on, information and communication
technologies throughout society, it appears indispensable to ensure that
cybersecurity becomes widespread in the organisational culture of the Portuguese
business fabric and in the entities, bodies and services that make up the Public
Administration.
In fact, the increase in the occurrence of cybersecurity incidents may compromise
national security and interest, entail danger to human life and losses of a financial
nature, as well as compromise the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the
information, networks and information systems of the Public Administration, of
critical infrastructure operators, of operators of essential services and of digital
service providers. In view of these threats and considering the provisions of the
Directive to be transposed, the regime approved by this Decree-Law significantly
expands the set of entities covered by the regime, prioritising, on the one hand, the
widespread prevention of cybersecurity risks, but grading the regulatory
requirements according to the size of the entity and the importance of its activity,
as well as favouring the proportionality of the applicable measures. Its scope of
application covers a significant part of the Public Administration, adapting the
regime to the size and type of the public entity concerned. It should also be noted
that, as permitted by the Directive to be transposed, the regime approved by this
Decree-Law excludes from its scope of application public entities in the fields of
national security, public security, defence and intelligence services, including
entities with criminal investigation responsibilities and criminal police bodies.
Among the relevant aspects of the regime approved by this Decree-Law is also the
deepening of three fundamental instruments for public cybersecurity policies: the
National Cyberspace Security Strategy, defining the national strategic priorities
and objectives in matters of cybersecurity; the National Plan for Response to
Large-Scale Cybersecurity Crises and Incidents, regulating and improving the
management of this type of incident; and the National Cybersecurity Reference
Framework, which will bring together and allow the dissemination of norms,
standards and good practices in the management of Cybersecurity.
Furthermore, the institutional framework of the regime approved by this Decree-
Law is broadened in relation to the previous regime, as required by the Directive to
be transposed. Accordingly, the National Cybersecurity Centre (CNCS) strengthens
its role as national cybersecurity authority, with particular note also of the
establishment of “sectoral” and “special” supervisory authorities, which exercise
supervision over specific sectors of the economy, thereby ensuring stability in the
supervision of each of the sectors covered, as well as relieving the cross-cutting
tasks entrusted to the CNCS.
At the inter-administrative level, the proposed model establishes an architecture of
convergence, cooperation and interoperability between the various national
entities competent in matters of cybersecurity and of internal and external security,
fostering, in particular, the cross-cutting nature of flows of relevant information
and the sharing of tactical contributions in incident response between the national
entities competent in matters of cybersecurity, with a view to maximising
Portuguese public capabilities for the prevention, early detection, mitigation,
repression and accountability of cyber threats.
The strengthening of cooperation with the private sector is another of the axes of
the institutional design provided for in the regime approved by this Decree-Law,
fostering collaboration between the competent authorities and private parties in
the various relevant matters.
As regards the risk-management model provided for in the regime approved by this
Decree-Law, it consists of setting pre-defined risk standards, applicable to each
sector and type of entity, and of applying corresponding prevention measures, with
the addition of an analysis of residual risk. This model makes it possible to relieve
the authorities of a case-by-case analysis of the risk of each entity covered, while
also making it easier for the entities covered to know the category in which they
fall and, thus, the minimum measures they must adopt.
In these terms, the proposed model introduces simplicity, predictability and a
better match between the mandatory measures and the threat landscape
applicable to each sector of activity.
On the other hand, the model fosters the creation of a market for cybersecurity
certification, which will have economic utility and will make it possible to
generalise a presumption of conformity of entities.
Finally, as regards the supervisory model provided for in the regime approved by
this Decree-Law, it, reflecting the provisions of the Directive to be transposed,
provides for a dual regime, differentiating the treatment to be given to essential
and important entities according to the cybersecurity risks associated with each
category, in compliance, once again, with the principle of proportionality.
This Decree-Law thus has as its objective the establishment of the new legal
framework applicable in matters of cybersecurity, without prejudice to the fact that
the entry into force of this regime necessarily implies a significant strengthening of
the capacity of the CNCS and a new reflection on its institutional framework.
This Decree-Law was submitted to public consultation between 22 November and
31 December 2024.
The self-governing bodies of the Autonomous Regions, the National Cybersecurity
Centre, the Portuguese Bar Association, the Superior Council of the Public
Prosecution Service, the National Data Protection Commission and the Commission
for Access to Administrative Documents were heard.

Article 1
Subject matter
1 — This Decree-Law approves the legal regime for cybersecurity, transposing into
the domestic legal order Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European Parliament and
of the Council, of 14 December 2022, on measures for a high common level of
cybersecurity across the Union, amending Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 and
Directive (EU) 2018/1972, and repealing Directive (EU) 2016/1148 (NIS 1
Directive).
2 — This Decree-Law further provides for:
a) The implementation, in the domestic legal order, of the obligations arising from
Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17
April 2019, on ENISA (the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity) and on
information and communications technology cybersecurity certification and
repealing Regulation (EU) No 526/2013 (Cybersecurity Act), implementing a
national cybersecurity certification framework;
b) The ninth amendment to the Internal Security Law, approved by Law No.
53/2008, of 29 August, as currently worded;
c) The second amendment to the Cybercrime Law, approved by Law No. 109/2009,
15 September, amended by Law No. 79/2021, of 24 November;
d) The second amendment to the Electronic Communications Law, approved by
Law No. 16/2022, of 16 August, amended by Decree-Law No. 114/2024, of 20
December.
3 — The provisions of this Decree-Law are without prejudice to the measures and
the legal framework in force intended to safeguard the essential functions of the
State, namely the measures and provisions concerning the preservation of national
security and interest, the production of intelligence for the internal and external
security of the Portuguese State, the protection of State secrets and classified
information, and also to safeguard the maintenance of public order and to allow the
investigation, detection and repression of criminal offences, without prejudice to
the provisions of Articles 7 and 8
Article 2
Legal regime for cybersecurity
The legal regime for cybersecurity is approved, in annex to this Decree-Law, of
which it forms an integral part.

Article 7
Addition to Law No. 109/2009, of 15 September
Article 8-A is added to the Cybercrime Law, approved by Law No. 109/2009, of 15
September, as currently worded, with the following wording:
“Article 8-A
Acts not punishable on grounds of public cybersecurity interest
1 — Facts liable to constitute the crimes of unlawful access and unlawful
interception provided for, respectively, in Articles 6 and 7 are not punishable if the
following circumstances are cumulatively met:
a) The agent acts with the sole intention of identifying the existence of
vulnerabilities in an information system, information and communication
technology products and services, which have not been created by the agent or by
a third party on whom the agent depends, and with the purpose of contributing,
through their disclosure, to the security of cyberspace;
b) The agent does not act with the purpose of obtaining an economic advantage or
a promise of economic advantage arising from the agent’s action, without prejudice
to the remuneration that the agent obtains in return for the agent’s professional
activity;
c) The agent communicates, immediately after the action, any vulnerabilities
identified to the owner or to the person designated by the owner to manage the
information system, information and communication technology product or service,
and to the holder of any data obtained that are protected under the legislation
applicable in matters of personal data protection, namely the General Data
Protection Regulation (GDPR), approved by Regulation (EU) 2016/679, of the
European Parliament and of the Council, of 27 April 2016, Law No. 26/2016, of 22
August, as currently worded, Law No. 58/2019, of 8 August, and Law No. 59/2019,
of 8 August;
d) The agent’s conduct is proportionate to its purposes and strictly limited by them,
confined to the actions necessary to identify the vulnerabilities and not causing:
i) A disruption or interruption of the functioning of the system or service
concerned;
ii) The deletion or deterioration of computer data or their unauthorised copying;
iii) Any prejudicial, damaging or harmful effect on the person or entity affected,
directly or indirectly, or on any third parties, excluding the effects corresponding to
the unlawful access or unlawful interception itself, under the terms provided for in
Articles 6 and 7, and also those that would already result, with high probability,
from the vulnerability detected itself or from its exploitation;
e) The agent’s conduct does not constitute a breach of personal data protected
under the legislation applicable in matters of personal data protection, namely
Regulation (EU) 2016/679, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 27
April 2016, Law No. 58/2019, of 8 August, and Law No. 59/2019, of 8 August.
2 — The communication provided for in point (c) of the preceding paragraph must
also be made to the national cybersecurity authority, which forwards it to the
Judiciary Police whenever it is of criminal relevance.
3 — For the purposes of determining the proportionality of the agent’s conduct,
account shall be taken of whether it was necessary for the detection of the
vulnerability and whether the extent of the systems or computer data accessed,
consulted and/or copied was required by the interest in contributing to the security
of cyberspace, the use of the following practices being expressly prohibited:
a) Denial-of-service (DoS) or distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) mechanisms;
b) Social engineering, defined as the act of deceiving persons responsible for or
users of information systems with a view to the disclosure of sensitive or
confidential information;
c) ‘Phishing’ and variants;
d) Theft or larceny of passwords or other sensitive information;
e) Wilful deletion or alteration of computer data;
f) Wilful infliction of damage on the information system;
g) Installation and distribution of malicious software.
4 — Without prejudice to the rules applicable in matters of data protection,
computer data that are communicated to the owner or to the person in charge of
managing the information system, information and communication technology
product and service, or to the national cybersecurity authority must be deleted
within 10 days counted from the moment the vulnerability is corrected, their secret
nature having to be guaranteed throughout the procedure.
5 — Likewise not punishable are facts committed with the consent of the owner or
administrator of an information system, information and communication technology
product or service, without prejudice to the duty to notify any vulnerabilities
identified to the national coordinating authority in charge of responding to
cybersecurity incidents of any vulnerabilities identified, under the terms provided
for in the legal regime for cybersecurity.”
Article 8
Transitional provision
1 — The entry into force of this Decree-Law does not affect the validity of the
decisions taken by the Security Assessment Commission under the previous
regime, which continue to produce effects for a period of 180 days after the date of
entry into force of this Decree-Law, during which a new security assessment must
be carried out.
2 — On the basis of the new security assessment referred to in the preceding
paragraph, and under the regime approved in annex to this Decree-Law, the
member of the Government responsible for the area of cybersecurity may decide on
the renewal, modification or replacement of the decisions adopted by the Security
Assessment Commission under the previous regime.
3 — The regulations and acts adopted by the National Communications Authority in
matters of security and integrity of networks and services, which have been
adopted under Law No. 5/2004, of 10 February, Law No. 16/2022, of 18 August,
and Decree-Law No. 65/2021, of 30 July, and which are not incompatible with the
provisions of this Decree-Law, remain in force until their replacement or revocation
by the competent entities.
Article 9
Repealing provision
The following are repealed:
a) Article 2-A of Decree-Law No. 3/2012, of 16 January, as currently worded, which
approves the organic structure of the National Security Office;
b) The legal regime for the security of cyberspace, approved by Law No. 46/2018,
of 13 August;
c) The regulations of the legal regime for the security of cyberspace, approved by
Decree-Law No. 65/2021, of 30 July;
d) Articles 59 to 65 and points (m) to (t) of paragraph 3 of Article 178 of the
Electronic Communications Law, approved by Law No. 16/2022, of 16 August, as
currently worded.
Article 10
Production of effects
1 — The provisions of point (d) of Article 9 produce effects after the replacement or
revocation, by the competent entities, of the regulations and acts adopted by
ANACOM in matters of security and integrity of networks and services, under Law
No. 5/2004, of 10 February, Law No. 16/2022, of 18 August, and Decree-Law No.
65/2021, of 30 July, and which are not incompatible with the provisions of this
Decree-Law.
2 — The provisions of paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 27, Articles 28 to 30, Article 33
and points (b), (c) and (f) of paragraph 1 of Article 61 of the legal regime for
cybersecurity, approved in annex to this Decree-Law, produce effects 24 months
after the publication of the regulations referred to in Articles 8, 14, 26, 31, 32 and
83 of the said regime.
Article 11
Entry into force
This Decree-Law enters into force 120 days after its publication.

Legal regime for cybersecurity
CHAPTER I
General provisions
Article 1
Subject matter
1 — This Decree-Law establishes the legal regime for cybersecurity, transposing
into the domestic legal order Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European
Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022, on measures for a high
common level of cybersecurity across the Union, amending Regulation (EU) No
910/2014 and Directive (EU) 2018/1972, and repealing Directive (EU) 2016/1148
(NIS 1 Directive).
2 — The provisions of this Decree-Law are without prejudice to compliance with
the provisions of the legislation applicable in matters of:
a) Criminal investigation proceedings by the judicial authorities and by the
competent criminal police bodies, namely by the Public Prosecution Service and by
the Judiciary Police;
b) Proceedings within the respective exclusive competences of the Security
Intelligence Service and of the Strategic Defence Intelligence Service in matters of
producing intelligence concerning the safeguarding of national independence,
national interests, the external and internal security of the Portuguese State, and
the prevention of sabotage, terrorism, espionage and the commission of acts which,
by their nature, may alter or destroy the constitutionally established rule of law;
c) Protection of personal data, namely within the scope of the General Data
Protection Regulation (GDPR), approved by Regulation (EU) 2016/679, of the
European Parliament and of the Council, of 27 April 2016, Law No. 26/2016, of 22
August, as currently worded, Law No. 58/2019, of 8 August, and Law No. 59/2019,
of 8 August;
d) Processing of personal data and protection of privacy in the electronic
communications sector, namely within the scope of the provisions of Law No.
41/2004, of 18 August, as currently worded;
e) Identification and designation of national and European critical infrastructures,
namely within the scope of Decree-Law No. 20/2022, of 28 January;
f) Combating the sexual abuse and sexual exploitation of children and child
pornography, namely within the scope of Law No. 103/2015, of 24 August;
g) Protection of users of essential public services, namely within the scope of Law
No. 23/96, of 26 July, as currently worded;
h) Security and emergency in the electronic communications sector, namely within
the scope of the provisions of the Electronic Communications Law, approved by
Law No. 16/2022, of 16 August, as currently worded;
i) State Secrets and Classified Information, namely within the scope of the
provisions of Organic Law No. 2/2014, of 6 August, amended by Law No. 1/2015, of
8 January.
Article 2
Definitions
For the purposes of this Decree-Law, the following definitions apply:
a) “Asset” means every information and communication system, the equipment and
the other physical and logical resources managed or held by the entity, which
support, directly or indirectly, one or more services;
b) “Competent cybersecurity authority” means the National Cybersecurity Centre
(CNCS) or, where applicable, the competent national sectoral cybersecurity
authority under point (a) of paragraph 2 of Article 15, without prejudice to the
reservations of exclusive competence of public entities with responsibilities in
matters of criminal investigation, production of intelligence and cyber defence;
c) “Cyber threat” means a cyber threat within the meaning of point 8 of Article 2 of
Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17
April 2019;
d) “Significant cyber threat” means a cyber threat which, based on its technical
characteristics, can be considered liable to have a severe impact on the network
and information systems of an entity or of the users of the entities’ services,
causing considerable material or non-material damage;
e) “Cybersecurity” means cybersecurity within the meaning of point 1 of Article 2
of Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17
April 2019;
f) “Entity” means a legal person created and recognised as such under the national
law of its place of establishment, which may, acting under its own name, exercise
rights and be subject to obligations;
g) “Entities competent in the field of cyberspace security” means the Cyber
Defence Operations Command of the General Staff of the Armed Forces, the
Judiciary Police, the Security Intelligence Service and the Strategic Defence
Intelligence Service;
h) “Entity providing domain name registration services” means a registrar or an
agent acting on behalf of registrars, such as a privacy or proxy registration service
provider or reseller;
i) “Technical specification” means a technical specification within the meaning of
point 4 of Article 2 of Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, of the European Parliament
and of the Council, of 25 October 2012;
j) “Incident” means an event compromising the availability, authenticity, integrity
or confidentiality of stored, transmitted or processed data or of the services offered
by, or accessible via, network and information systems;
k) “Large-scale cybersecurity crisis or incident” means an incident which causes a
level of disruption that exceeds the response capacity of the Portuguese State,
which has a significant impact on at least two Member States of the European
Union, or which, owing to its reach and systemic impact, calls for urgent cross-
sectoral coordination;
l) “Significant incident” means an incident which:
i) Causes, or is liable to cause, severe operational disruption of the services or
financial loss for the entity concerned;
ii) Affects, or is liable to affect, other natural or legal persons by causing
considerable material or non-material damage;
m) “Risk matrix” means the reference framework establishing the risk values for
the set of risk scenarios that bear on a sector and subsector of activity, considering
the common assets and the main threats and vulnerabilities;
n) “Cybersecurity risk-management measures or cybersecurity measures” means
measures of a technical, operational and organisational scope, aimed at managing
the risks posed to the security of the network and information systems used in their
operations or in the provision of their services, as well as preventing or minimising
the impact of incidents on the recipients of their services and on other services;
o) “Online marketplace” means an online marketplace within the meaning of point
(n) of Article 3 of Decree-Law No. 57/2008, of 26 March, as currently worded,
which establishes the regime applicable to unfair commercial practices;
p) “Online search engine” means an online search engine within the combined
meaning of point 5 of Article 2 of Regulation (EU) 2019/1150, of the European
Parliament and of the Council, of 20 June 2019, and point (j) of Article 3 of
Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, of the Parliament and of the Council, of 19 October
2022;
q) “Standard” means a standard within the meaning of point 1 of Article 2 of
Regulation (EU) No 1025/2012, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of
25 October 2012;
r) “Cybersecurity operations” means actions to operationalise the cybersecurity
risk-management measures;
s) “Research organisation” means an entity whose primary goal is to conduct
applied research or experimental development with a view to exploiting the results
of that research for commercial or scientific purposes;
t) “Social networking services platform” means an online platform, defined in
accordance with point (i) of Article 3 of Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, of the
European Parliament and of the Council, of 19 October 2022, that enables end-
users to connect, share, discover and communicate with each other across multiple
devices, in particular via chats, posts, videos and recommendations;
u) “Internet exchange point” means a network facility which:
i) Enables the interconnection of more than two independent networks
(autonomous systems), primarily for the purpose of facilitating the exchange of
Internet traffic;
ii) Interconnects only autonomous systems;
iii) Does not require Internet traffic passing between any pair of participating
autonomous systems to pass through any third autonomous system, nor does it
alter or otherwise interfere with such traffic;
v) “DNS service provider” means an entity that provides publicly available
recursive domain name resolution services for Internet end-users or authoritative
domain name resolution services for third-party use, with the exception of root
name servers;
w) “Trust service provider” means a trust service provider within the meaning of
point 19 of Article 3 of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, of the European Parliament
and of the Council, of 23 July 2014, as amended by Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of
the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022, and by
Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 11
April 2024;
x) “Managed security service provider” means a managed service provider that
carries out or provides assistance for activities relating to cybersecurity risk
management;
y) “Managed service provider” means an entity that provides services related to
the installation, management, operation or maintenance of ICT products, networks,
infrastructure, applications or any other network and information systems, via
assistance or active administration carried out either on customers’ premises or
remotely;
z) “Qualified trust service provider” means a qualified trust service provider within
the meaning of point 20 of Article 3 of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, of the
European Parliament and of the Council, of 23 July 2014, as amended by Directive
(EU) 2022/2555, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December
2022, and by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, of the European Parliament and of the
Council, of 11 April 2024;
aa) “ICT process” means an ICT process within the meaning of point 14 of Article 2
of Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17
April 2019;
bb) “ICT product” means an ICT product within the meaning of point 12 of Article 2
of Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17
April 2019;
cc) “Near miss” means an event that could have compromised the availability,
authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of stored, transmitted or processed data or
of services offered by, or accessible via, network and information systems, but that
was successfully prevented or did not materialise;
dd) “Content delivery network” means a network of geographically distributed
servers for the purpose of ensuring high availability, accessibility or fast delivery of
digital services and content to Internet users on behalf of content and service
providers;
ee) “Top-level domain name registry” or “TLD (top level domain, as the English
expression and acronym) name registry” means an entity which has been delegated
a specific TLD and is responsible for administering it, including the registration of
domain names under the TLD and the technical operation of that TLD, including
the operation of its name servers, the maintenance of its databases and the
distribution of TLD zone files across name servers, irrespective of whether any of
those operations are carried out by the entity itself or are outsourced, but
excluding situations where TLD names are used by a registry only for its own use;
ff) “Public electronic communications network” means a public electronic
communications network within the meaning of point (oo) of paragraph 1 of Article
3 of the Electronic Communications Law, approved by Law No. 16/2022, of 16
August, as currently worded;
gg) “Network and information systems” means:
i) An electronic communications network within the meaning of point (mm) of
paragraph 1 of Article 3 of the Electronic Communications Law, approved by Law
No. 16/2022, of 16 August, as currently worded;
ii) A device or a group of interconnected or related devices, one or more of which,
pursuant to a programme, carry out automatic processing of digital data; or
iii) Digital data stored, processed, retrieved or transmitted by elements referred to
in the preceding sub-points for the purposes of their operation, use, protection and
maintenance;
hh) “Representative” means any natural or legal person established in the
European Union, explicitly designated to act on behalf of a DNS service provider, a
top-level domain name registry, an entity providing domain name registration
services, a cloud computing service provider, a data centre service provider, a
content delivery network provider, a managed service provider, a managed security
service provider, or a provider of online marketplaces, of online search engines or
of social networking services platforms that is not established in the European
Union, which may be contacted by the competent entities in place of the
represented entity with regard to the obligations incumbent on the latter under
this Decree-Law;
ii) “Risk” means the measure of the potential for loss or disruption caused by an
incident, resulting from the combination of the magnitude of such loss or
disruption and the likelihood of occurrence of the incident;
jj) “Residual risk” means the measure of risk existing after the adoption of the
minimum cybersecurity measures;
kk) “Security of network and information systems” means the ability of network
and information systems to resist, at a given level of confidence, events liable to
compromise the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of the stored,
transmitted or processed data, or of the services offered by, or accessible via, those
network and information systems;
ll) “Data centre service” means a service that encompasses structures, or groups of
structures, dedicated to the centralised accommodation, interconnection and
operation of IT and network equipment providing data storage, processing and
transport services, together with all the facilities and infrastructures for power
distribution and environmental control;
mm) “Cloud computing service” means a digital service that enables on-demand
administration and broad remote access to a scalable and elastic pool of shareable
computing resources, including where such resources are distributed across
several locations;
nn) “Electronic communications service” means an electronic communications
service within the meaning of point (ss) of paragraph 1 of Article 3 of the
Electronic Communications Law, approved by Law No. 16/2022, of 16 August, as
currently worded;
oo) “Trust service” means a trust service within the meaning of point 16 of Article
3 of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, of the European Parliament and of the Council,
of 23 July 2014, as amended by Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European
Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022, and by Regulation (EU)
2024/1183, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 11 April 2024;
pp) “Qualified trust service” means a qualified trust service within the meaning of
point 17 of Article 3 of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, of the European Parliament
and of the Council, of 23 July 2014, as amended by Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of
the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022, and by
Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 11
April 2024;
qq) “ICT service” means an ICT service within the meaning of point 13 of Article 2
of Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17
April 2019;
rr) “Domain name system” or “DNS” means a hierarchical distributed naming
system which enables the identification of Internet services and resources,
allowing end-user devices to use Internet routing and connectivity services to
reach those services and resources;
ss) “Digital service” means a service within the meaning of point (g) of Article 3 of
Decree-Law No. 30/2020, of 29 June, which lays down the rules governing the
procedure for the provision of information in the field of technical regulations
relating to products and of rules relating to information society services;
tt) “Incident handling” means all actions and procedures aiming to prevent, detect,
analyse, contain or respond to an incident and to recover from an incident;
uu) “Vulnerability” means a weakness, susceptibility or flaw affecting network and
information systems, information or communication technology (ICT) products or
services, which can be exploited by a cyber threat.
Article 3
Personal scope of application
1 — This Decree-Law applies to private entities of one of the types listed in
Annexes I or II to this Decree-Law, which form an integral part of it, which, subject
to the territorial scope criteria laid down in the following article:
a) Qualify as medium-sized enterprises under Article 2 of Annex III to this Decree-
Law, which forms an integral part of it, corresponding to the provisions of
Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, of 6 May 2003, or exceed the ceilings
for medium-sized enterprises provided for in paragraph 1 of that article; and
b) Provide their services or carry out their activities in the European Union.
2 — This Decree-Law also applies to entities of one of the types listed in Annexes I
or II to this Decree-Law which, regardless of their nature and size and subject to
the territorial scope criteria laid down in the following article, meet at least one of
the following requirements:
a) The entity concerned is:
i) A provider of public electronic communications networks or a provider of
publicly available electronic communications services;
ii) A trust service provider;
iii) A top-level domain name registry, a domain name registration service provider,
and a domain name system service provider;
b) The entity concerned is the sole provider of a service which is essential for the
maintenance of critical societal or economic activities, namely the activities
corresponding to the sectors, subsectors and types of entities referred to in
Annexes I and II to this Decree-Law;
c) A disruption of the service it provides could have a considerable impact on
public safety, public security or public health;
d) A disruption of the service it provides could induce considerable systemic risks,
in particular for sectors where such disruption could have a cross-border impact;
e) The entity is critical because of its specific importance at national or regional
level for the particular sector or type of service, or for other interdependent
sectors.
3 — This Decree-Law applies to the Public Administration, covering:
a) The services of the direct administration of the State, central and peripheral;
b) The services of the direct administration of the Autonomous Regions, central
and peripheral;
c) The entities of the indirect administration of the State;
d) The entities of the indirect administration of the Autonomous Regions;
e) The entities of the autonomous administration;
f) The independent administrative bodies and entities, with the exception of Banco
de Portugal, the Securities Market Commission and the Insurance and Pension
Funds Supervisory Authority.
4 — This Decree-Law applies to the following entities:
a) The Office of the Ombudsman;
b) The Economic and Social Council;
c) The technical and administrative services of the Presidency of the Republic, of
the Assembly of the Republic, of the courts and of the registries with competence
for processing procedures, of the Superior Council of the Judiciary, of the Superior
Council of the Administrative and Tax Courts and of the Superior Council of the
Public Prosecution Service, without prejudice to the provisions of paragraph 7.
5 — This Decree-Law applies to entities which, regardless of their size, are
identified as critical entities under the provisions of Directive (EU) 2022/2557, of
the European Parliament and the Council, of 14 December 2022, on the resilience
of critical entities, without prejudice to point (f) of paragraph 3.
6 — This Decree-Law applies to higher education institutions.
7 — This Decree-Law does not apply:
a) To the General Staff of the Armed Forces and of the branches of the Armed
Forces, as regards the network and information systems directly related to their
command and control;
b) To public entities with criminal investigation responsibilities and to criminal
police and public security bodies, as regards the network and information systems
directly related to their command and control;
c) To public entities with exclusive responsibilities in matters of production of
intelligence, namely the Intelligence System of the Portuguese Republic, the
Strategic Defence Intelligence Service and the Security Intelligence Service, as
regards the network and information systems directly related to their command
and control;
d) To public entities whose activity concerns network and information systems
directly related to the production and dissemination of classified information,
namely with national markings, of the North Atlantic Treaty Organisation (NATO),
and of the European Union, or catalogued as a State secret, as regards those
network and information systems;
e) To other public entities that carry out their activity in the fields of national
security, public security, including entities with criminal investigation
responsibilities and criminal police bodies, defence and intelligence services, as
regards the network and information systems directly related to the activities of
production of intelligence and prevention, investigation, detection and repression
of criminal offences;
f) To private entities that provide services exclusively to one or more of the entities
provided for in the preceding points and as regards those activities.
8 — This Decree-Law applies to the entities referred to in point (b) of paragraph 2
of Article 15 only as regards the exercise of their competences in their capacity as
special national cybersecurity authorities.
9 — This Decree-Law is without prejudice to the provisions of Regulation (EU)
2022/2554, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022,
on digital operational resilience for the financial sector.
Article 4
Territorial delimitation of the personal scope of application
1 — This Decree-Law applies to the entities referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 of
the preceding article which:
a) Have an establishment in the national territory;
b) In the case of undertakings providing public electronic communications
networks or providing publicly available electronic communications services, make
them available in the national territory;
c) In the case of domain name system service providers, top-level domain name
registries, entities providing domain name registration services, cloud computing
service providers, data centre service providers, content delivery network
providers, managed service providers, managed security service providers, as well
as providers of online marketplaces, of online search engines or of social
networking services platforms:
i) Have their main establishment in the national territory;
ii) Not having an establishment in the European Union, their representative has an
establishment in the national territory.
2 — For the purposes of sub-point (i) of point (c) of the preceding paragraph, the
entity is considered to have its main establishment in the national territory when:
a) The decisions related to the cybersecurity risk-management measures are
predominantly taken in the national territory;
b) The cybersecurity operations are carried out in the national territory, if it is not
possible to determine whether the decisions related to the cybersecurity risk-
management measures were predominantly taken there or in another Member
State of the European Union;
c) The establishment of the entity with the highest number of employees is located
in the national territory, if it is not possible to determine whether the cybersecurity
operations are carried out there.
3 — Under the terms of Article 20, the CNCS, in the face of a request for mutual
assistance from another Member State of the European Union and in relation to an
entity referred to in point (c) of paragraph 1, may, within the limits of that request,
take appropriate supervisory and enforcement measures in relation to the entity
concerned.
Article 5
Extraterritorial scope
1 — In order to prevent significant cyber threats to the security of the network and
information systems of a large number of users, the CNCS may, after hearing the
Higher Council for Cyberspace Security (CSSC), adopt corrective or restrictive
enforcement measures, including an order to suspend the service in the national
territory, addressed to a service provider without an establishment or
representation in the national territory that does not offer adequate cybersecurity
measures.
2 — Except where the measures are urgent, the CNCS presents a preliminary
statement of reasons for the decisions to the service provider, granting a period for
reply of not less than 10 days.
3 — For the purposes of determining and stating the reasons for the enforcement
measures provided for in the preceding paragraphs, the CNCS shall take into
consideration the actions and measures, as well as their effectiveness and extent,
taken by European and international cybersecurity authorities.
4 — The competent cybersecurity authority, under the terms of its competences
and to the extent necessary, may, in relation to an entity with a relevant connection
to the national territory, provide assistance to the competent authorities of the
Member States of the European Union, at their reasoned request, namely by means
of:
a) Provision of information in relation to a supervisory or enforcement measure
taken in relation to that entity, through the respective single point of contact;
b) Application of supervisory or enforcement measures under the provisions of
Chapter VI, if necessary jointly with the competent authority of the respective
Member State of the European Union;
c) Provision of support to the competent authority of the respective Member State
of the European Union as regards its application of supervisory or enforcement
measures, and such support may include the forms of assistance referred to in the
preceding points.
5 — The competent cybersecurity authority may only refuse the assistance
requested under the preceding paragraph if it exceeds its competences, is
disproportionate in relation to its supervisory tasks or compromises essential
interests of the Portuguese State in matters of national security, public security or
defence.
Article 6
Essential entities and important entities
1 — For the purposes of this Decree-Law, the following are considered essential
entities:
a) Entities of one of the types referred to in Annex I to this Decree-Law which
exceed the ceilings for medium-sized enterprises provided for in Article 2 of Annex
III to this Decree-Law, corresponding to those of Commission Recommendation
2003/361/EC, of 6 May 2003;
b) Qualified trust service providers and top-level domain name registries, and
domain name system service providers, regardless of their size;
c) Undertakings providing public electronic communications networks or publicly
available electronic communications services which are considered medium-sized
enterprises under Article 2 of Annex III to this Decree-Law, corresponding to those
of Commission Recommendation 2003/361/EC, of 6 May 2003;
d) Public Administration entities whose attributions include the provision of
services in the areas of development, maintenance and management of information
and communication technology infrastructures, or those that present a particularly
high degree of digital integration in the provision of their services, and also the
public entity responsible for the area of educational assessment;
e) Entities identified as critical entities under Directive (EU) 2022/2557, of the
European Parliament and the Council, of 14 December 2022, on the resilience of
critical entities and repealing Council Directive 2008/114/EC, regardless of their
size;
f) Any other entity of one of the types listed in Annexes I or II to this Decree-Law,
referred to in points (b) to (e) of paragraph 2 of Article 3, which is qualified as an
essential entity on the basis of the entity’s respective degree of exposure to risks,
the size of the entity and the likelihood of occurrence of incidents and their
severity, including their social and economic impact.
2 — For the purposes of this Decree-Law, important entities are the entities of the
types referred to in Annexes I and II to this Decree-Law which are not considered
essential entities under the preceding paragraph.
3 — For the purposes of this Decree-Law, important entities are also the entities of
one of the types listed in Annexes I or II to this Decree-Law, referred to in points
(b) to (e) of paragraph 2 of Article 3, which justify such qualification on the basis of
the entity’s respective degree of exposure to risks, the size of the entity and the
likelihood of occurrence of incidents and their severity, including their social and
economic impact.
4 — The assignment of the qualifications of essential entities and important entities
provided for in the preceding paragraphs results from the mechanisms provided for
in Article 8
Article 7
Relevant public entities
1 — Public entities that are not qualified as essential or important entities under
the preceding article are considered relevant public entities, being placed in two
groups for the purposes of the application of specific regimes under this Decree-
Law and the other regulations issued by the CNCS.
2 — The following are considered Group A relevant public entities:
a) The services of the direct administration of the State, central and peripheral,
with 250 or more workers on their staff establishment;
b) The services of the direct administration of the Autonomous Regions, central
and peripheral, with 250 or more workers on their staff establishment;
c) The entities of the indirect administration of the State, with more than 250
workers on their staff establishment;
d) The entities of the indirect administration of the Autonomous Regions, with
more than 250 workers on their staff establishment;
e) The entities of the autonomous administration, with more than 250 workers on
their staff establishment;
f) Public business entities that exceed the ceilings provided for in Article 2 of
Annex III to this Decree-Law, corresponding to those of Commission
Recommendation 2003/361/EC, of 6 May 2003;
g) Independent administrative entities;
h) The Economic and Social Council, the Office of the Ombudsman, the technical
and administrative services of the Presidency of the Republic, of the Assembly of
the Republic, of the courts and of the registries with competence for processing
procedures, of the Superior Council of the Judiciary, of the Superior Council of the
Administrative and Tax Courts and of the Superior Council of the Public
Prosecution Service.
3 — The following are considered Group B relevant public entities:
a) The services of the direct administration of the State, central and peripheral,
that have between 75 and 249 workers on their staff establishment;
b) The services of the direct administration of the Autonomous Regions, central
and peripheral, that have between 75 and 249 workers on their staff establishment;
c) The entities of the indirect administration of the State, that have between 75 and
249 workers on their staff establishment;
d) The entities of the indirect administration of the Autonomous Regions, that have
between 75 and 249 workers on their staff establishment;
e) The entities of the autonomous administration, that have between 75 and 249
workers on their staff establishment;
f) Public business entities qualified as medium-sized enterprises under Annex III to
this Decree-Law, corresponding to those of Commission Recommendation
2003/361/EC, of 6 May 2003.
4 — The assignment of the qualification of relevant public entity provided for in the
preceding paragraphs results from the qualification mechanisms provided for in
the following article.
Article 8
Procedure for the qualification of entities
1 — The entities provided for in Article 3 identify themselves on an electronic
platform made available by the CNCS, within 30 days after the start of their
activity or, if the entity is already in activity upon the entry into force of this
Decree-Law, within 60 days after that electronic platform is made available, being
responsible for keeping that information duly updated.
2 — The qualification of entities by the CNCS on the basis of the criteria provided
for in points (a) to (c) and (e) of paragraph 1 and in paragraph 2 of Article 6, and
also in Article 7, results from the mechanism provided for in the preceding
paragraph.
3 — The qualification of entities by the CNCS on the basis of the criteria provided
for in points (d) and (f) of paragraph 1 and in paragraph 3 of Article 6 is
communicated at least 60 days in advance to the member of the Government
responsible for the area of cybersecurity and reviewed at least every two years.
4 — The qualification provided for in the preceding paragraph is duly reasoned by
the CNCS, being preceded by a prior hearing of the entity concerned and, where
applicable, by an opinion of the national sectoral cybersecurity authorities referred
to in point (a) of paragraph 2 of Article 15
5 — The CNCS or, where applicable, the national sectoral cybersecurity authorities
competent under point (a) of paragraph 2 of Article 15, notifies the entity of its
qualification under paragraphs 2 and 3, within a maximum period of 30 days from
the date of that qualification.
6 — Domain name registration service providers must identify themselves and
communicate the information provided for in paragraph 2 of Article 35 through the
electronic platform made available by the CNCS, within 30 days after the start of
their activity.
7 — The operating rules of the electronic platform referred to in this article are
defined by means of a regulation to be approved by the CNCS.
8 — The qualification procedure referred to in this article is without prejudice, for
the entities covered, to compliance with the duty provided for in Article 35
Article 9
Concurrence of qualifications and cybersecurity measures
1 — Where an entity simultaneously falls within more than one qualification, the
regime that proves most demanding for managing the risks posed to the security of
network and information systems applies, in accordance with the following order:
a) Essential entities;
b) Important entities;
c) Group A relevant public entities;
d) Group B relevant public entities.
2 — The CNCS may associate with the qualification of the entity, under the
provisions of paragraph 4 of Article 26 and of Article 33, cybersecurity measures
and other technical and organisational measures resulting from the instruments
provided for in this Decree-Law, non-compliance with which may determine the
application of the corresponding sanctions under the sanctions regime provided for
in Chapter VII.
Article 10
Processing of personal data
1 — The entities that make up the institutional framework for cyberspace security,
under Article 15, process personal data to the extent strictly necessary to ensure
compliance with legal obligations and the pursuit of missions in the public interest
or in the exercise of official authority vested in them, under the provisions of points
(c) or (e) of paragraph 1 and of paragraph 3 of Article 6 of the GDPR and in
accordance with this Decree-Law and other applicable national legislation.
2 — Without prejudice to the provisions of Article 29 of Law No. 58/2019, of 8
August, the entities that make up the institutional framework for cyberspace
security may process special categories of personal data in order, to the extent
strictly necessary and under point (g) of paragraph 2 of Article 9 of the GDPR, to:
a) Prevent the materialisation of a significant cyber threat to the security of
network and information systems;
b) Respond effectively to a cybersecurity incident.
CHAPTER II
Structuring instruments
Article 11
Structuring instruments of Cyberspace Security
The following are structuring instruments of Cyberspace Security, in compliance
with the applicable national and international legal and regulatory provisions:
a) National Cyberspace Security Strategy (ENSC);
b) National plan for response to large-scale cybersecurity crises and incidents;
c) National Cybersecurity Reference Framework (QNRCS);
d) National Cyber Defence Strategy;
e) Strategic Concept of National Defence.
Article 12
National Cyberspace Security Strategy
1 — The ENSC defines the framework, the priorities, the national strategic
objectives and a governance framework defining the roles and responsibilities of
the stakeholders at national level with relevance for the implementation of the
ENSC.
2 — The ENSC includes, namely:
a) The objectives and priorities of the ENSC, covering, namely, the sectors in
Annexes I and II to this Decree-Law;
b) A governance framework to achieve the objectives and priorities referred to in
the preceding point;
c) A governance framework defining the roles and responsibilities of the
stakeholders at national level with relevance for the implementation of the ENSC
and consolidating institutional cooperation and coordination under this Decree-
Law;
d) A mechanism to identify relevant assets and an assessment of the risks in
Portugal;
e) The identification of the preparedness, response and recovery measures in the
event of incidents, including cooperation between the public and private sectors;
f) A list of the various authorities and stakeholders involved in the implementation
of the ENSC;
g) A policy framework for enhanced cooperation between the competent
authorities under this Decree-Law and the competent authorities resulting from the
transposition of Directive (EU) 2022/2557, of the European Parliament and of the
Council, of 14 December 2022, for the purposes of sharing information on risks,
cyber threats and incidents, as well as on non-cyber risks, threats and incidents,
and of the exercise of supervisory tasks;
h) A plan, including the necessary measures, to enhance the general level of
cybersecurity and cyber hygiene education, training and awareness of citizens;
i) A plan, including the necessary measures, appropriate to the specific
cybersecurity needs of small and medium-sized enterprises, qualified under Article
2 of Annex III to this Decree-Law, corresponding to those of Commission
Recommendation 2003/361/EC, of 6 May 2003;
j) The promotion of the development, research and integration of advanced
technologies aimed at implementing innovative measures, good practices and
controls, including the use of artificial intelligence, in matters of cybersecurity risk
management and of detection and prevention of cyberattacks.
3 — The ENSC is approved by resolution of the Council of Ministers, on a proposal
from the CNCS, after hearing the CSSC and after a period of public consultation of
not less than 30 days.
4 — The approval referred to in the preceding paragraph is also preceded by an
examination of the ENSC by the Assembly of the Republic.
5 — The ENSC is reviewed and updated every five years, following an evaluation
process based on key impact and performance indicators, and this period may be
shortened by decision of the member of the Government responsible for the area of
cybersecurity upon a reasoned proposal from the CNCS.
6 — The ENSC is without prejudice to the approval by the competent entities,
where necessary, of instruments establishing sectoral cybersecurity strategies,
which must be reviewed and updated on the same terms as apply to the ENSC.
Article 13
National plan for response to large-scale cybersecurity crises and incidents
1 — The national plan for response to large-scale cybersecurity crises and incidents
establishes the objectives and arrangements for the management of this type of
crises and incidents.
2 — The national plan for response to large-scale cybersecurity crises and incidents
is approved by resolution of the Council of Ministers, on a joint proposal from the
Secretary-General of the Internal Security System, the Judiciary Police, the
Security Intelligence Service, the Strategic Defence Intelligence Service, the Cyber
Defence Operations Command and the CNCS, the latter being responsible for its
implementation, follow-up and monitoring, in close collaboration with the entities
that make up the crisis unit provided for in paragraph 4 of Article 16 of Law No.
53/2008, of 29 August, in the wording given to it by this Decree-Law, and after
hearing the CSSC.
3 — The national plan for response to large-scale cybersecurity crises and incidents
must ensure consistency with the existing frameworks for general crisis
management at national level.
Article 14
National Cybersecurity Reference Framework
1 — The QNRCS is the national reference instrument for the identification of the
existing norms, standards and good practices in matters of cybersecurity and
information security management.
2 — The QNRCS is approved by regulation of the CNCS, after hearing the CSSC,
and must be regularly updated, at least every five years.
3 — Essential and important entities must take the QNRCS into account in the
context of the adoption of the cybersecurity measures provided for in Articles 27 et
seq.
4 — The national sectoral cybersecurity authorities referred to in point (a) of
paragraph 2 of Article 15 may adopt norms complementary to the QNRCS, by
means of their own regulation, in coordination with the CNCS.
5 — Without prejudice to the preceding paragraphs, the application of the QNRCS
by essential, important and relevant public entities is the subject of a regulation to
be approved by the CNCS, providing for specific cybersecurity measures and
conformity levels.
CHAPTER III
Institutional framework for cyberspace security
Article 15
Organisation
1 — The institutional framework for cyberspace security is composed of the
following entities:
a) The CSSC, in its capacity as advisory body to the Prime Minister in the field of
cybersecurity;
b) The CNCS, in its capacity as:
i) National cybersecurity authority;
ii) Single point of contact for the purposes of cooperation within the European
Union and at international level, without prejudice to the competences assigned to
other entities in matters of international cooperation;
iii) National cybersecurity certification authority;
iv) Entity forming part of the national cybersecurity incident response team;
c) The Secretary-General of the Internal Security System, in the capacity of
national authority for the management of large-scale cybersecurity crises and
incidents.
2 — The institutional framework for cyberspace security also includes:
a) In the capacity of national sectoral cybersecurity authorities:
i) The National Security Office (GNS), as regards trust services for electronic
transactions in the internal market;
ii) The National Communications Authority (ANACOM), as regards the matter of
electronic communications and postal services;
b) In the capacity of special national cybersecurity authorities, as regards the
matter of digital operational resilience of the financial sector:
i) The Insurance and Pension Funds Supervisory Authority;
ii) The Securities Market Commission;
iii) Banco de Portugal.
c) The Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission;
d) The Judiciary Police;
e) The Security Intelligence Service;
f) The Strategic Defence Intelligence Service;
g) The Cyber Defence Operations Command.
3 — The organisation of the institutional framework for cyberspace security is
without prejudice to the informal coordination of the authorities referred to in this
article, namely through participation in multilateral coordination fora as regards
the defence of cyberspace security, such as the Office of Cyberspace Liaison
Officers for tactical-operational cooperation (G5).
Article 16
Higher Council for Cyberspace Security
1 — The CSSC is the strategic coordination body that supports the Prime Minister
in matters of cyberspace security.
2 — The CSSC is composed of:
a) The Prime Minister, who chairs it, or the member of the Government responsible
for the area of cybersecurity with delegated competence;
b) Two Members of Parliament designated by the Assembly of the Republic using
the d’Hondt method;
c) The Secretary-General of the Internal Security System;
d) The Secretary-General of the Intelligence System of the Portuguese Republic;
e) The director of the Security Intelligence Service;
f) The director of the Strategic Defence Intelligence Service;
g) The director-general of the National Security Office;
h) The coordinator of the CNCS;
i) The ambassador for cyber diplomacy;
j) The head of the Communications and Information, Cyberspace and Space Centre
of the General Staff of the Armed Forces;
k) The director of the National Unit for Combating Cybercrime and Technological
Crime of the Judiciary Police;
l) A representative of the Public Prosecution Service, designated by the Prosecutor
General of the Republic;
m) The president of the National Council for Civil Emergency Planning;
n) The president of the board of directors of the Agency for the Technological
Reform of the State, I. P.;
o) A representative of the National Network of Computer Security Incident
Response Teams;
p) The highest-ranking officer of the national sectoral and special cybersecurity
authorities, referred to in paragraph 2 of Article 15, not listed in the preceding
points.
3 — The CSSC is also composed of a representative of the Regional Government of
the Azores and a representative of the Regional Government of Madeira.
4 — The chair, on his or her own initiative or at the request of any of the members
of the CSSC, may convene other holders of public bodies or invite other entities
and personalities of recognised merit to participate in meetings.
5 — The chair is replaced in his or her absences and impediments by the member
of the Government whom he or she designates.
Article 17
Competences of the Higher Council for Cyberspace Security
1 — The competences of the CSSC are:
a) To ensure strategic coordination for cyberspace security;
b) To issue a prior opinion on the ENSC, as well as to monitor its implementation
and to draw up annually, or whenever necessary, an evaluation report on it;
c) To issue a prior opinion on the national plan for response to large-scale
cybersecurity crises and incidents;
d) To issue opinions on matters relating to cyberspace security, at the request of
the Prime Minister, or of the member of the Government to whom he or she
delegates, within the scope of its competences;
e) To respond to requests from the Prime Minister, or from the member of the
Government to whom he or she delegates, within the scope of its competences;
f) To propose to the member of the Government responsible for the area of
cybersecurity that security assessments be carried out, under the provisions of the
following article.
2 — The annual report evaluating the implementation of the ENSC is sent to the
Assembly of the Republic by 30 June of the year following that to which it refers.
3 — The Intelligence Services brief the CSSC on the assessment of the current
threat to national cyberspace and to international cyberspace, whenever
appropriate or whenever the threat level assigned by the Security Intelligence
Service is revised.
Article 18
Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission
1 — The Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission operates alongside the
CSSC and is responsible for carrying out security assessments of information and
communication technology equipment, components or services, used in public or
private network and information systems, from manufacturers or suppliers that
may be considered high-risk for the security of cyberspace of national interest,
namely in the contexts of internal and external security, national defence, the
integrity of the democratic process and other sovereign functions, and also of the
operation of critical infrastructures and the provision of essential services.
2 — The Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission has the following
composition:
a) The director-general of the National Security Office, who chairs it;
b) The coordinator of the CNCS;
c) A representative of ANACOM;
d) A representative of the Internal Security System;
e) A representative of the Intelligence System of the Portuguese Republic;
f) The ambassador for cyber diplomacy;
g) A representative of the Judiciary Police;
h) A representative of the Security Intelligence Service;
i) A representative of the Strategic Defence Intelligence Service;
j) A representative of the Cyber Defence Operations Command;
k) A representative of the Directorate-General for Foreign Policy;
l) A representative of the Directorate-General for National Defence Policy;
m) A representative of the Competition Authority.
3 — The member of the Government responsible for the area of cybersecurity may
determine the application of provisional restrictions on the use, the cessation of
use or the exclusion of information and communication technology equipment,
components or services, used in public or private network and information systems,
considered high-risk for the security of cyberspace of national interest, on a
proposal from the Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission, based on a
security assessment carried out under the provisions of the following paragraphs.
4 — The security assessment must be duly reasoned, taking into account the
technical risks of the equipment, components or services, their context of use or
and the exposure of their manufacturers or suppliers to undue influence from
foreign countries, considering for that purpose, namely, relevant information issued
by the competent national and European Union entities or contained in the national
or European assessments of risk to the security of network and information
systems, as well as other relevant security risks.
5 — In order to assess the level of exposure of manufacturers or suppliers to undue
influence from a foreign country, the following elements may be considered:
a) The manufacturer or supplier being subject, directly or indirectly, to interference
by the government or administration of a foreign country;
b) The manufacturer or supplier being domiciled in, or in any way relevantly linked
to, countries recognised by Portugal, by the European Union, by the Organisation
for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) or by NATO as responsible
for or involved in actions hostile to the internal security and national defence of
Portugal or of its allies, namely acts of espionage or sabotage;
c) The manufacturer or supplier being domiciled in, or in any way relevantly linked
to, countries that do not have legislation or diplomatic agreements with Portugal or
with the European Union in matters of data protection, cybersecurity and
protection of intellectual property;
d) The manufacturer or supplier being associated with practices of introducing
vulnerabilities or hidden access;
e) The manufacturer or supplier adopting corporate governance models that do not
clarify the degree of influence of, or linkage to, foreign countries in the conditions
of the preceding points;
f) The production and supply chains of the manufacturer or supplier showing
systemic control and security failures.
6 — Security assessments may be carried out or reviewed at the request of the
member of the Government responsible for the area of cybersecurity, as well as, in
application of the Portuguese mechanism for safeguarding essential strategic
assets, at the request of the member of the Government responsible for the area in
which the strategic asset concerned is integrated.
7 — The proposal of the Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission made
following the security assessment must, in its content, scope and intensity, respect
the principle of proportionality, considering, namely, the degree of risk established,
the degree of incidence, global and specific, on each item of equipment, component
or service concerned, the loss suffered by the affected manufacturer and supplier,
and also the economic and social losses potentially arising from the decision.
8 — The Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission may request any entity,
public or private, to provide any information necessary for the preparation of
security assessments.
9 — The decision of the member of the Government responsible for the area of
cybersecurity provided for in paragraph 3 defines the reasonable time limits and,
where applicable, the geographical scope of the measure to be applied, so that the
public or private entities concerned proceed with its implementation.
10 — The documents or information produced in the course of the work of the
Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission are considered classified information
at the “restricted” security level, unless the chair of the Commission considers it
necessary to assign a higher security classification level, and without prejudice to
these documents or information being classified as State secrets under Organic
Law No. 2/2014, of 6 August, as currently worded.
11 — In the exercise of its competences, the CNCS or, where applicable, the
national sectoral or special national authority, monitors compliance with the
requests of the Cyberspace Security Assessment Commission and with the decision
of the member of the Government responsible for the area of cybersecurity
provided for in this article, sanctioning non-compliance under point (d) of
paragraph 1 of Article 63 and point (a) of paragraph 1 of Article 61, respectively.
12 — The technical, administrative and logistical support of the Cyberspace
Security Assessment Commission, as well as the respective associated costs, are
provided and borne by the GNS.
Article 19
National Cybersecurity Centre
1 — The CNCS is the national cybersecurity authority, its mission being to ensure
that the country achieves and maintains a high level of cybersecurity, through the
promotion of the continuous improvement of national cybersecurity and of
international cooperation, as well as through the definition and implementation of
the measures and instruments necessary for the anticipation, detection, reaction
and recovery from situations which, in view of the imminence or occurrence of
incidents, jeopardise the national interest and the functioning of essential entities,
important entities and relevant public entities.
2 — The CNCS is also the single point of contact for the purposes of cooperation at
European Union level, as well as at international level, in matters of cybersecurity,
without prejudice to the competences assigned to other authorities in matters of
cooperation in criminal matters, namely the competences of the Judiciary Police for
international cooperation conferred on it by the provisions of Articles 20 to 26 and
29 of the Cybercrime Law, and in matters of production of intelligence concerning
the internal and external security of the Portuguese State and of its allies.
3 — The CNCS incorporates the “CERT.PT”, provided for in Article 22, which acts
as the national cybersecurity incident response team.
4 — The CNCS is likewise the national cybersecurity certification authority, namely
for the purposes of the provisions of Article 58 of Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the
European Parliament and of the Council, of 17 April 2019, without prejudice to the
competences of the GNS as regards the certification and accreditation of
information and communication systems that process classified information, under
Decree-Law No. 3/2012, of 16 January, as currently worded.
Article 20
Competences of the National Cybersecurity Centre
1 — The CNCS, within the scope of the responsibilities assigned in paragraphs 1
and 2 of Article 19, pursues the attributions and exercises the competences
described in the following points:
a) To develop the national capabilities for prevention, monitoring, detection,
reaction, analysis and correction intended to deal with cybersecurity incidents,
cyberattacks and cyber threats;
b) To cooperate with the entities competent in the field of cyberspace security
within the scope of their respective attributions;
c) To communicate, within 24 hours, to the Judiciary Police all facts of criminal
relevance of which it becomes aware in the course of its activity;
d) To communicate, within 24 hours, to the Security Intelligence Service all facts
concerning threats to internal security, cyber espionage and cyber sabotage, of
which it becomes aware in the course of its activity;
e) To adopt regulations and issue guidelines, recommendations and technical
instructions relating to cybersecurity;
f) To propose to the member of the Government responsible for the area of
cybersecurity the definition of the national cybersecurity alert level, developed
through the CNCS’s own regulation and disseminated in coordination with the
entities competent in the field of cyberspace security, and to issue orders and
instructions appropriate to the severity of the situation;
g) To inform the Secretary-General of the Internal Security System of the
verification of a significant cyber threat or of the occurrence of a large-scale
cybersecurity crisis or incident, under points (d) and (k) of Article 2, respectively
and without prejudice to the provisions of paragraph 2 of Article 21;
h) To issue orders, guidelines, recommendations and technical instructions in
matters of coordinated vulnerability disclosure;
i) To prevent and mitigate the impact of cybersecurity incidents, namely through
the detection and disclosure of vulnerabilities in network and information systems,
in collaboration with public and private entities and natural and legal persons;
j) To apply the supervisory and enforcement measures under the provisions of
Chapter VI;
k) To issue warnings, namely about vulnerabilities, relating to malware or other
cybersecurity risks in ICT products, components or services;
l) To ensure cross-border cooperation with the competent authorities of the
Member States of the European Union, with the European Commission, with the
European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and other institutions, bodies
and agencies of the European Union that carry out activities in the field of
cybersecurity and of the competences entrusted to it by this article, namely
participation and national representation in multilateral and bilateral fora with its
counterparts, without prejudice to the provisions of Articles 20 to 26 and 29 of the
Cybercrime Law, including participation and national representation:
i) In the Cooperation Group, provided for in Article 14 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555,
of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022;
ii) In the European Network of CSIRTs (Computer Security Incident Response
Team, as the English expression and acronym), provided for in Article 15 of
Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14
December 2022; and
iii) In the Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network (EU-CyCLONe) provided for
in Article 16 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European Parliament and of the
Council, of 14 December 2022;
m) To issue a non-binding opinion, when requested, on any legislative measure
relating to cybersecurity;
n) To promote awareness, training and qualification of human resources in the area
of cybersecurity, with a view to forming a knowledge community and a national
culture of cybersecurity and cyber hygiene;
o) To support the development of technical, scientific and industrial capabilities,
promoting innovation and development projects in the area of cybersecurity;
p) To publish studies and reports in the area of cybersecurity;
q) To approve the forms that prove necessary appropriate for the exercise of its
attributions.
2 — The CNCS has, in the exercise of the responsibilities assigned by paragraph 4
of Article 19, pursues the attributions and exercises the competences described in
the following points:
a) To request from conformity assessment bodies, holders of cybersecurity
certificates and issuers of statements of conformity the information it needs for the
exercise of its competences;
b) To take appropriate measures to ensure that conformity assessment bodies,
holders of national or European cybersecurity certificates and issuers of
statements of conformity comply with the provisions of the legislation applicable in
matters of cybersecurity certification;
c) To exercise the other competences legally established for cybersecurity
certification authorities, namely those arising from Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of
the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17 April 2019, without prejudice to
the competences of the GNS as regards the certification and accreditation of
information and communication systems that process classified information, under
Decree-Law No. 3/2012, of 16 January, as currently worded;
d) To implement a national cybersecurity certification framework, establishing the
provisions necessary for the preparation, implementation and execution of
certification schemes, to which the provisions of Title III of Regulation (EU)
2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17 April 2019, apply,
with the necessary adaptations;
e) To evaluate specific certification schemes, namely as to their adequacy,
coordination with the Portuguese Accreditation Institute, I. P., in its capacity as
national accreditation body, as well as with the Portuguese Quality Institute, I. P., in
its capacity as national standardisation body, and with the other public entities with
competences within the scope of the subject matter covered by the certification;
f) To develop and implement specific cybersecurity certification schemes relating to
information and communication technology entities, products, services and
processes that are not yet covered by a European scheme, whenever the specificity
of the object of certification so justifies;
g) To promote the training of auditors in the field of cybersecurity, in collaboration
with the Portuguese Accreditation Institute, I. P.
3 — Any regulatory cybersecurity provision issued by the national sectoral or
special cybersecurity authorities is preceded by an opinion of the CNCS.
4 — Public and private entities provide their collaboration to the CNCS for the
exercise of its respective attributions and competences under this Decree-Law, in
compliance with the principle of proportionality.
5 — The duty of cooperation provided for in the preceding paragraph may include
physical access to the entities’ premises for the performance of steps forming part
of supervisory or incident response actions, without prejudice to compliance with
access requirements provided for in other special information security regimes and
subject to the requirements provided for in the Code of Administrative Procedure.
6 — The CNCS acts in close cooperation with the national structures responsible
for cyber espionage, cyber defence, cybercrime and cyber terrorism.
Article 21
Cybersecurity crisis management authority
1 — The Secretary-General of the Internal Security System is the national authority
for the management of large-scale cybersecurity crises and incidents, also referred
to as the cybersecurity crisis management authority.
2 — The declaration of large-scale cybersecurity crises and incidents depends on
the assignment of a high threat level by the Security Intelligence Service, under
the terms provided for in the plan for coordination, control and operational
command of the Security Forces and Services, approved by Council of Ministers
Deliberation No. DB 14/2010, of 5 March, or on the communication, by the CNCS,
of the occurrence of a large-scale cybersecurity crisis or incident, under point (g)
of paragraph 1 of the preceding article.
3 — The Secretary-General of the Internal Security System convenes the
cybersecurity crisis unit, under paragraph 4 of Article 16 of Law No. 53/2008, of 29
August, as currently worded.
Article 22
Cybersecurity Incident Response Team
1 — The “CERT.PT” is the national cybersecurity incident response team.
2 — The “CERT.PT” is integrated in the CNCS and has technical and operational
autonomy.
3 — The “CERT.PT” exercises the following competences:
a) To ensure the operational response to incidents;
b) To monitor and analyse cyber threats, vulnerabilities and incidents at national
level and, upon request, to provide assistance to essential, important and relevant
public entities regarding real-time or near real-time monitoring of their network
and information systems;
c) To activate rapid alert mechanisms, send alert messages, make communications
and disseminate information to essential, important and relevant public entities, to
competent authorities and to other stakeholders, on cyber threats, vulnerabilities
and incidents, including in real time;
d) To intervene in the event of incidents and provide assistance to essential,
important and relevant public entities, namely, where applicable, by proposing to
the CNCS the issuing of operational orders, instructions and guidelines as to
measures to be adopted to contain, mitigate and resolve the incidents, as well as
the appropriate time limits for their implementation;
e) In situations of serious and proven risk, to propose to the competent
cybersecurity authority the adoption of enforcement measures necessary for an
immediate response to the cyber threat, incident or crisis, under paragraph 3 of
Article 52, if the essential, important or relevant public entity concerned does not
do so voluntarily;
f) To collect and analyse forensic data, determine their preservation and carry out
dynamic risk and incident analysis and develop situational awareness in matters of
cybersecurity;
g) To carry out, at the request of an essential, important or relevant public entity, a
proactive scan of the entity’s respective network and information systems, in order
to detect vulnerabilities with a potential significant impact;
h) To implement tools and functionalities that enable the secure sharing of
information with essential, important and relevant public entities, as well as with
other stakeholders;
i) To carry out, on its own initiative, proactive and non-intrusive scans of publicly
accessible network and information systems of essential, important and relevant
public entities, with the aim of detecting vulnerable or insecure network and
information systems and informing the entities concerned, insofar as they have no
negative impact on the functioning of those entities’ services;
j) To promote the adoption and use of common or standardised practices;
k) To ensure national representation in the network of national cybersecurity
incident response teams under sub-point (ii) of point (l) of paragraph 1 of Article 20
and other international fora for cooperation of cybersecurity incident response
teams;
l) To participate in national fora for cooperation of computer security incident
response teams;
m) To participate in national and international events and training actions;
n) To collaborate and coordinate its action with sectoral, national and European
CSIRT networks, whenever necessary or appropriate;
o) To cooperate with the entities competent in the field of cyberspace security.
4 — In the exercise of its competences, the “CERT.PT” may determine the
prioritisation of certain tasks through a risk-based approach, considering, namely,
the current threat assessment produced by the Security Intelligence Service.
5 — Public and private entities provide their collaboration to the “CERT.PT” for the
exercise of its respective attributions and competences under this Decree-Law.
6 — The collaboration referred to in the preceding paragraph may include physical
access to premises and the sharing of information between the entities that provide
incident response services to third parties and the “CERT.PT”, and joint actions, on
the latter’s initiative, for the purposes of point (e) of paragraph 3.
Article 23
Cooperation between national authorities
1 — The CNCS, the Secretary-General of the Internal Security System and the
national sectoral cybersecurity authorities, in the exercise of their attributions and
competences under this Decree-Law, act in close cooperation with:
a) The National Data Protection Commission (CNPD), whenever incidents that have
given rise to personal data breaches are at issue, under Article 79;
b) The Public Prosecution Service, the courts and the Judiciary Police, whenever
incidents that may have given rise to the commission of cybercrimes are at issue,
namely through:
i) The communication, as soon as possible, of facts relating to the preparation and
execution of cybercrimes of which they have become aware in the exercise of their
functions, without prejudice to the provisions of Article 38;
ii) The performance of the necessary and urgent precautionary acts to ensure the
preservation of evidence and the sharing, under the legal terms, of other evidential
elements necessary for the strict exercise of the competences provided for in
points (a) to (e) of paragraph 3 of the preceding article;
iii) The performance of the role of expert provided for in Article 153 of the Code of
Criminal Procedure;
c) The Cyber Defence Operations Command, namely when incident prevention,
monitoring, detection, reaction, analysis and correction within the scope of cyber
defence and of the cybersecurity of the Armed Forces are at issue;
d) The Security Intelligence Service, namely in the sharing of information
necessary for the preservation of the security of cyberspace of national interest, in
particular as regards espionage, sabotage, terrorism and organised crime.
2 — The obtaining of information under the cooperation provided for in the
preceding paragraph must respect the legislation applicable in matters of personal
data protection, namely the GDPR, Law No. 26/2016, of 22 August, as currently
worded, Law No. 58/2019, of 8 August, and Law No. 59/2019, of 8 August.
3 — The cooperation provided for in point (b) of paragraph 1 does not affect
judicial secrecy.
4 — Access to information under the cooperation provided for, namely, in sub-
points (i) and (ii) of point (b) of paragraph 1, relating to proceedings under
investigation, may be refused on the grounds provided for in paragraph 1 of Article
89 of the Code of Criminal Procedure.
5 — The Judiciary Police and the Security Intelligence Service each designate a
permanent liaison officer to the CNCS.
6 — The terms of technical and operational cooperation between the CNCS, the
Cyber Defence Operations Command, the Judiciary Police, the Security Intelligence
Service and the Strategic Defence Intelligence Service are defined by mutual
agreement within the G5.
7 — The authorities referred to in this article must respond to requests for
information within five days after the date on which the information was requested,
save for duly justified reasons.
Article 24
Cooperation with the private sector
1 — The entities that form part of the institutional framework for cyberspace
security, under Article 15, must establish cooperative relations with the entities
covered by this Decree-Law and, where relevant, with other interested entities
from the private sector, with a view to achieving the objectives of the legal regime
for cybersecurity.
2 — The cooperative relations must cover at least the following aspects relating to
information sharing, adoption of good practices, and development or improvement
of common or standardised classification systems and taxonomies as regards:
a) Cybersecurity risk-management measures;
b) Indicators of exposure to risks or cyber threats;
c) Incident handling procedures;
d) Crisis management; and
e) Coordinated vulnerability disclosure, under Article 38
3 — In order to promote the exchange of knowledge, the sharing of good practices
and the mobilisation of specialised knowledge of private sector entities in support
of the competent cybersecurity authority, public-private partnerships for
cybersecurity may be adopted, defining the scope and the parties involved, the
governance model, the available funding options and the interaction between the
participating parties.
4 — Cybersecurity information-sharing arrangements may be concluded between
the entities referred to in paragraph 1 as well as, where relevant, with their
suppliers or service providers, for the following purposes:
a) To prevent, detect, respond to and recover from incidents or to mitigate their
impact;
b) To enhance the level of cybersecurity, in particular by raising awareness of cyber
threats, limiting or impeding their ability to spread, supporting a range of
defensive capabilities, vulnerability remediation and disclosure, threat detection,
containment and prevention techniques, mitigation strategies or response and
recovery stages, or promoting collaborative cyber-threat research between public
and private entities.
5 — The signatory parties to the information-sharing arrangements, where
necessary, take measures to protect the sensitive nature of the information shared
and to limit its distribution, in accordance with the so-called TLP (Traffic Light
Protocol, as the English expression and acronym).
6 — Essential and important entities are obliged to notify the competent
cybersecurity authority of their participation in the arrangements referred to in
paragraph 4, upon their conclusion, or, where applicable, of their withdrawal from
such arrangements, as soon as that withdrawal takes effect.
7 — The arrangements referred to in paragraph 4, when concluded by essential
and important entities covered by Regulation (EU) 2022/2554, of the European
Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022, on digital operational
resilience for the financial sector, are communicated to the respective special
national cybersecurity authorities.
8 — The CNCS ensures and manages an online platform for information sharing.
CHAPTER IV
Cybersecurity risk management and other duties
SECTION I
Cybersecurity and information security management
Article 25
Obligations of the management, direction and administration bodies
1 — The management, direction and administration bodies of essential and
important entities:
a) Approve the cybersecurity risk-management measures, adopted in accordance
with Article 27;
b) Oversee the implementation of the cybersecurity risk-management measures;
c) Ensure compliance with the supervisory and enforcement measures referred to
in Chapter VI;
d) Ensure that cybersecurity training actions are carried out on a regular basis, in
order to promote an internal management culture regarding cybersecurity risk-
management practices.
2 — The holders of the management, direction and administration bodies may be
held liable for an act or omission, with intent or gross negligence, under the
applicable legislation, for the infringements provided for in this Decree-Law.
3 — The responsibility and powers necessary for compliance with the obligations
referred to in this article may not be delegated, except to one of the holders of the
management, direction and administration bodies.
Article 26
Cybersecurity risk-management system
1 — Essential and important entities are responsible for ensuring the security of
network and information systems, taking appropriate technical, operational and
organisational measures to manage the risks posed to the security of the network
and information systems which they use for their operations and to prevent or
minimise the impact of incidents on recipients of their services and on other
services.
2 — The cybersecurity measures adopted must be based on a systemic approach
covering all risks to essential and important entities and aimed at protecting
against incidents all assets that guarantee the continuity of the functioning of the
networks and the information systems supporting essential services, including their
physical environment.
3 — The measures must also:
a) Ensure a level of security of network and information systems appropriate to the
risk concerned, taking into account the state of the art and, where applicable,
relevant European and international standards, as well as the cost of
implementation and its financial viability; and
b) Be proportionate to the degree of the entity’s exposure to risks, the size of the
entity and the likelihood of occurrence of incidents and their severity, including
their social and economic impact, according to the technical criteria that may be
defined by the CNCS.
4 — In order to guide the cybersecurity risk-management policy of essential and
important entities, the CNCS may issue technical harmonisation instructions and,
whenever necessary, draw up and update the risk matrix applicable to those
entities.
5 — Considering the sector of activity and the size of the entity and the risk matrix
defined, the CNCS, by means of a regulation to be approved by the CNCS, defines
minimum and specific cybersecurity measures and conformity levels to be adopted
by essential entities and important entities.
6 — The minimum cybersecurity measures are without prejudice to the adoption of
other measures that are necessary and proportionate, as a result of the analysis
and management of residual cybersecurity risks, under the following article.
7 — Relevant public entities must adopt the appropriate technical, operational and
organisational measures determined by the CNCS, according to the group to which
they belong, under Article 33
8 — Whenever the qualification of entities under Article 8, or the minimum
cybersecurity measures provided for in paragraph 5 of this article, are changed as
a result of the updating of the risk matrix, essential and important entities have a
period of six months to adapt to the new qualification or to the new minimum
cybersecurity measures, and this period may be extended up to one year by the
National Cybersecurity Authority, upon a reasoned request from the entity.
Article 27
Cybersecurity measures
1 — The cybersecurity measures to be adopted by essential and important entities,
taking into consideration the risk matrix within which they fall under the preceding
article, cover, namely, the following areas:
a) Incident handling;
b) Business continuity, such as backup management and disaster recovery, and
crisis management;
c) Supply chain security, including security-related aspects concerning the
relationships between each entity and its direct suppliers or service providers;
d) Security in the acquisition, development and maintenance of network and
information systems, including vulnerability handling and disclosure;
e) Policies and procedures to assess the effectiveness of cybersecurity risk-
management measures;
f) Basic cyber hygiene practices and cybersecurity training, including the holders
of the highest management bodies and workers;
g) Policies and procedures regarding the use of cryptography and, where
appropriate, encryption, without prejudice to the competences conferred on other
entities in matters of cryptography at national level or before other international
organisations of which Portugal is a member;
h) Human resources security, access control policies and asset management;
i) The use of multi-factor authentication or continuous authentication solutions,
secured communications and secured emergency communication systems within
the entity.
2 — Essential and important entities must also adopt, without undue delay, all
necessary, appropriate and proportionate corrective cybersecurity measures that
are indispensable to remedy failures or omissions in compliance with the measures
provided for in the preceding paragraph.
3 — The national sectoral cybersecurity authorities may issue regulatory provisions
for the adoption of sector-specific cybersecurity measures, without prejudice to the
provisions of paragraph 3 of Article 20
Article 28
Supply chain
The cybersecurity measures relating to supply chain security, including security-
related aspects concerning the relationships between each entity and its direct
suppliers or service providers, must consider, namely:
a) The vulnerabilities specific to each direct supplier and each service provider;
b) The overall quality of products in the cybersecurity component;
c) The cybersecurity practices of their suppliers and service providers, including
their secure development procedures;
d) The coordinated security risk assessments of critical ICT product, ICT system or
ICT service supply chains carried out under Article 22 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555,
of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022;
e) The decisions relating to the application of restrictions on the use, the cessation
of use or the exclusion of information and communication technology equipment,
components or services, under the provisions of paragraph 3 of Article 18
Article 29
Management of residual risk
1 — Essential and important entities must carry out a risk analysis and
management in relation to all assets that guarantee the continuity of the
functioning of the network and information systems they use, including the assets
that guarantee the provision of essential services, with the periodicity and the
technical and documentary elements to be defined by regulation of the competent
cybersecurity authority, in addition to compliance with the minimum cybersecurity
measures under paragraph 5 of Article 26
2 — On the basis of the risk analysis and management referred to in the preceding
paragraph, essential and important entities must adopt appropriate and
proportionate cybersecurity measures in order to manage the risks posed to the
security of the network and information systems they use, including residual risks,
taking into account the QNRCS, the state of the art and, where applicable, relevant
European and international standards.
3 — Essential and important entities must document the preparation, execution
and presentation of the results of the risk analysis.
Article 30
Annual report
1 — Essential and important entities must prepare and maintain an annual report
containing the following elements in relation to the calendar year to which they
refer:
a) A summary description of the main activities carried out in matters of security of
networks and information services;
b) Quarterly statistics of all incidents, indicating the number and type of incidents;
c) Aggregate analysis of incidents with a significant impact, with information on:
i) Number of users affected by the service disruption;
ii) Duration of the incidents;
iii) Geographical distribution, as regards the area affected by the incidents,
including an indication of cross-border impact;
d) Recommendations of activities, measures or practices that promote the
improvement of the security of network and information systems;
e) Problems identified and measures implemented following the incidents;
f) Any other information considered relevant.
2 — Essential entities send the annual report to the competent cybersecurity
authority, duly signed by the cybersecurity officer, on the following terms:
a) The first annual report is submitted:
i) By the last working day of the month of January of the calendar year following
the first calendar year of activity, where the activity began in the first half of the
year;
ii) By the last working day of the month of January of the second calendar year
following the first calendar year of activity, where the activity began in the second
half of the year;
b) Subsequent annual reports are submitted by the last working day of the month
of January of the calendar year following the year to which they refer.
3 — For the purposes of the provisions of sub-point (ii) of point (a) of the preceding
paragraph, the annual report must also cover the period between the date of start
of activity and the end of the calendar year preceding the one to which it refers.
4 — Important entities must communicate the annual report to the CNCS whenever
requested.
5 — The CNCS, after hearing the national sectoral cybersecurity authorities, may
adopt templates for the submission of the report referred to in the preceding
paragraphs.
Article 31
Cybersecurity officer
1 — Essential and important entities designate a cybersecurity officer for the
management of cybersecurity and information security, who is a holder of the
management, direction or administration bodies or reports to them organically and
directly.
2 — The cybersecurity officer has at least the following functions:
a) To propose the cybersecurity risk-management measures, including at supply
chain level, which must be approved under point (a) of paragraph 1 of Article 25;
b) To provide information concerning the cybersecurity risk-management measures
to the bodies of the entity responsible for overseeing them under point (b) of
paragraph 1 of Article 25;
c) To assist the bodies of the entity in complying with the supervisory and
enforcement measures under point (c) of paragraph 1 of Article 25;
d) To contribute to the promotion of a cybersecurity culture in the entity,
proposing, namely, the cybersecurity training actions provided for in point (d) of
paragraph 1 of Article 25;
e) To ensure the risk management provided for in Article 29;
f) To ensure compliance with the obligations concerning the preparation of the
annual report under Article 30;
g) To coordinate the actions of the permanent point of contact, provided for in
Article 32, when this function is not ensured by the officer.
3 — Essential and important entities communicate to the competent cybersecurity
authority, within 20 working days of the start of functions, the person designated to
exercise the functions of cybersecurity officer, including the information referred to
in a regulation to be approved by the CNCS.
4 — Essential and important entities that started activity before the date of entry
into force of this Decree-Law make the communication provided for in the
preceding paragraph within 20 working days of that date.
5 — Essential and important entities communicate, without undue delay, to the
competent cybersecurity authorities, the replacement of the cybersecurity officer.
6 — In relation to essential and important entities belonging to the direct
administration, the same cybersecurity officer may be designated for several
ministries, government areas or regional secretariats.
7 — In relation to essential and important entities within the same business group,
each undertaking may establish a member who functions as point of contact for
cybersecurity under the coordination of a security officer common to the group.
8 — The exercise of the functions of cybersecurity officer is compatible with the
accumulation of other functions within the same entity, without prejudice to the
provisions of this article.
Article 32
Permanent point of contact
1 — Essential and important entities ensure the function of permanent point of
contact with continuous availability 24 hours a day and seven days a week, limited
to activation periods, started and ended by communication from the competent
cybersecurity authority.
2 — Essential and important entities communicate to the CNCS at least one
permanent point of contact, which may be ensured by one member or by a team, so
as to ensure:
a) Information flows at operational and technical level with the competent
cybersecurity authority, namely:
i) Cross-sectoral coordination, including the effectiveness of the response to
security incidents with an impact at sector level;
ii) The obtaining of operational and technical information, following a notification
of incidents with a significant impact submitted by the same or by another entity;
iii) The obtaining and updating of integrated situational information in the context
of a significant incident;
b) Information sharing with the competent cybersecurity authority, when civil
protection emergency plans directly related to, or with an impact on, cybersecurity
are activated, as well as plans within the scope of cybersecurity civil emergency
planning, security plans for national or European critical infrastructures, or
resilience plans of national or European critical entities;
c) The operationalisation of the procedures laid down within the scope of a civil
protection emergency plan when they have an impact on the functioning of
network and information systems, or of cybersecurity civil emergency planning;
d) The receipt of the guidelines, recommendations, technical instructions and
orders issued by the competent cybersecurity authority.
3 — Essential and important entities must indicate to the competent cybersecurity
authority, within 20 working days of the start of functions, the person or persons
making up the team that ensure the functions of permanent point of contact, as
well as the respective main and alternative means of contact, containing the
information referred to in a regulation to be approved by the CNCS.
4 — Essential and important entities that started activity before the date of entry
into force of this Decree-Law must make the communication provided for in the
preceding paragraph within 20 working days of that date.
5 — Essential and important entities must immediately communicate to the
competent cybersecurity authority any change to the information provided for in
paragraph 3.
6 — Essential and important entities must ensure that the permanent point of
contact has main and alternative means of contact for communication with the
competent cybersecurity authority.
Article 33
Cybersecurity measures applicable to relevant public entities
1 — Relevant public entities must comply with the cybersecurity measures
established by the CNCS under the following paragraph.
2 — The CNCS establishes, by means of a regulation, the cybersecurity measures
that must be complied with by relevant public entities, considering the criteria
provided for in paragraphs 2 and 3 of Article 26 and in terms proportionate and
appropriate to the group to which they belong, in accordance with Article 7
3 — Relevant public entities are subject to the supervisory and enforcement
measures provided for in Articles 55 and 56, respectively.
4 — The provisions of paragraph 8 of Article 26 apply to relevant public entities.
Article 34
Cybersecurity certification
1 — The CNCS may require essential, important and relevant public entities to
obtain certification, national, European or international, attesting compliance with
the cybersecurity measures of this Decree-Law, namely in accordance with
certification schemes drawn up on the basis of Portuguese Normative Document —
Technical Specification (DNP TS) 4577-1, Digital Maturity — Digital Seal, and of
the QNRCS, ensuring, in any case, an equivalence matrix with existing reference
certification schemes.
2 — The CNCS may also require essential, important and relevant public entities,
under paragraph 1 of Article 24 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the Parliament and
of the Council, of 14 December 2022, to use ICT products, services and processes,
developed by the entity or supplied by third parties, certified under national and
European cybersecurity certification schemes adopted under Article 49 of
Regulation (EU) 2019/881, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 17
April 2019.
3 — Essential, important and relevant public entities may use the certification
referred to in paragraph 1 to demonstrate the implementation of appropriate and
proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures to manage the
security risks of the network and information systems used in their respective
operations or provision of services.
SECTION II
Other duties
Article 35
Duty of registration
1 — For registration purposes, essential, important and relevant public entities
have the duty to enter on the electronic platform referred to in paragraph 7 of
Article 8 the elements that allow their complete identification, namely:
a) Name of the entity concerned;
b) Tax identification number;
c) Address and up-to-date contact details, including email addresses, IP ranges and
telephone numbers;
d) Where applicable, the relevant sector and subsector referred to in Annexes I or
II to this Decree-Law; and
e) Where applicable, a list of the Member States of the European Union in which
they provide services falling within the scope of this Decree-Law.
2 — In addition to the data referred to in the preceding paragraph, the top-level
domain name registry, as well as entities that are DNS service providers, domain
name registration service providers, cloud computing service providers, data
centre service providers, content delivery network providers, managed service
providers, managed security service providers, as well as providers of online
marketplaces, of online search engines and of social networking services platforms,
have the duty to enter on the electronic platform referred to in paragraph 7 of
Article 8 the following elements:
a) The address of its main establishment and of the other legal establishments it
has in the European Union or, if it is not established in the Union, of the designated
representative;
b) Up-to-date contact details, including email addresses and telephone numbers of
the entity and, where applicable, of its designated representative;
c) The Member States where it provides services; and
d) The IP ranges.
3 — Essential, important and relevant public entities and domain name registration
service providers notify the CNCS of any change to the data referred to in the
preceding paragraphs, within 30 working days of the change.
4 — In the case of the TLD name registry, as well as entities that are DNS service
providers, domain name registration service providers, cloud computing service
providers, data centre service providers, content delivery network providers,
managed service providers, managed security service providers, as well as
providers of online marketplaces, of online search engines and of social networking
services platforms, the change to the data referred to in paragraphs 1 and 2 is
notified within three months of the change.
Article 36
Database of domain name registration data
1 — The top-level domain name registry and the entities providing domain name
registration services must collect and maintain accurate and complete domain
name registration data in databases created specifically for that purpose.
2 — The collection and maintenance of the data referred to in the preceding
paragraph constitutes a legal obligation within the meaning and for the purposes of
point (c) of paragraph 1 of Article 6 of the GDPR.
3 — The database referred to in paragraph 1 contains the following information:
a) The domain name;
b) The date of registration;
c) The name, contact email address and telephone number of the registrant;
d) The contact address and contact telephone number of whoever administers the
domain name, if different from the registrant.
4 — The top-level domain name registry and the entities providing domain services
adopt policies and procedures, including verification procedures, to ensure that
their respective databases, under paragraph 1, contain accurate and complete
information.
5 — The domain name registration data and the policies and procedures referred to
in the preceding paragraphs must be publicly available, where they are not
personal data and are not protected under the legislation applicable in matters of
personal data protection, namely the GDPR, Law No. 26/2016, of 22 August, as
currently worded, Law No. 58/2019, of 8 August, and Law No. 59/2019, of 8
August.
Article 37
Access to domain name registration data
1 — The top-level domain name registry and the entities providing domain name
registration services guarantee free access to specific domain name registration
data to whoever submits a duly reasoned request for access and meets the legal
requirements, in accordance with the legislation applicable in matters of personal
data protection, namely the GDPR, Law No. 26/2016, of 22 August, as currently
worded, Law No. 58/2019, of 8 August, and Law No. 59/2019, of 8 August.
2 — The requests for access referred to in the preceding paragraph are granted
within 72 hours of their receipt.
CHAPTER V
Incident prevention and handling
SECTION I
Prevention and monitoring of vulnerabilities
Article 38
Vulnerabilities in information systems
1 — The “CERT.PT” is the national coordinating entity for the purposes of
coordinated disclosure of vulnerabilities affecting network and information systems
and information and communication technology products, components and
services.
2 — The “CERT.PT” acts as a trusted intermediary, facilitating the interaction
between the reporting natural or legal person and the manufacturer or supplier of
ICT products or provider of ICT services that are potentially vulnerable, at the
request of either party.
3 — The functions of the “CERT.PT” include, namely:
a) The identification and contacting of the entities referred to in the preceding
paragraph;
b) The provision of support to natural or legal persons reporting vulnerabilities;
c) The negotiation of the disclosure timeline and the management of vulnerabilities
that affect multiple entities.
4 — The “CERT.PT” preserves the anonymity of any natural or legal person
reporting a vulnerability, if that person so requests, without prejudice to the
provisions of the Cybercrime Law, approved by Law No. 109/2009, of 15
September, in the wording introduced by this Decree-Law.
5 — The data included in the communications made under this article must be
deleted within 10 days, counted from the moment the vulnerability is corrected,
their confidentiality having to be guaranteed throughout the procedure.
Article 39
Communication of vulnerabilities
Where the vulnerability may have a significant impact on entities in more than one
Member State of the European Union, the “CERT.PT” cooperates with its
counterparts, both within the European Network of CSIRTs and within EU-
CyCLONe.
SECTION II
Notification of incidents
Article 40
Mandatory notification
1 — Essential, important and relevant public entities notify any significant incident
to the competent cybersecurity authority.
2 — Compliance with mere notification does not give rise to increased liability for
the notifying entity.
3 — In order to determine whether an incident has a significant impact under
paragraph 1, the entities concerned must take into consideration, namely, the
following parameters:
a) Number of users affected by the service disruption;
b) Total number of users of the disrupted service;
c) The duration of the incident;
d) The level of severity of the disruption of the functioning of the service;
e) The extent of the impact on economic and social activities.
4 — The entities must also take into consideration the parameters and thresholds
defined, where applicable, by technical instruction of the CNCS and by the
implementing acts of the Commission provided for in paragraph 11 of Article 23 of
Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14
December 2022.
5 — Compliance with the provisions of this Decree-Law does not exempt from
compliance with the specific incident notification obligations on the terms defined
by the authorities competent for that purpose, namely the Public Prosecution
Service, the Judiciary Police, the CNPD, the State Secrets Oversight Entity and the
GNS, in accordance with the applicable legal and regulatory provisions.
6 — Notifications must be submitted on the electronic platform referred to in
paragraph 7 of Article 8
7 — Essential, important and relevant public entities are assured the possibility of
notifying an incident simultaneously to the competent cybersecurity authority, to
the special cybersecurity authorities, and to the entities provided for in paragraph
5, through the platform provided for in paragraph 7 of Article 8, on terms to be
defined by protocol concluded between the said authorities.
Article 41
Types of notifications
1 — For each incident subject to mandatory notification, essential, important and
relevant public entities submit:
a) An initial notification, under Article 42;
b) A notification of the end of the significant impact, under Article 43;
c) A final report, under Articles 44
2 — In cases where the incident is resolved within two hours of its detection, the
said entities are only obliged to send the notification of the end of the significant
impact.
3 — Without prejudice to the provisions of the preceding paragraph, essential,
important and relevant public entities may also be notified to submit an
intermediate report, under Article 44
4 — The format and procedure for the notification of incidents and the taxonomy of
incidents, including the categories of causes of incidents and their effects, are
defined by technical instruction of the CNCS, without prejudice to the
implementing acts adopted by the Commission provided for in paragraph 11 of
Article 23 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European Parliament and of the
Council, of 14 December 2022.
Article 42
Initial notification
1 — The initial notification must be sent to the competent cybersecurity authority
as soon as the essential, important or relevant public entity concludes that there is,
or may come to be, a significant incident, without undue delay and within 24 hours
after that finding, except where this is incompatible with the mitigation or
resolution of the incident.
2 — The initial notification must include at least the following information:
a) Name, telephone number and email address of a representative of the entity,
where different from the permanent point of contact referred to in Article 32, for
the purpose of possible contact by the competent cybersecurity authority;
b) Date and time of the start or, where it is impossible to determine it, of the
detection of the incident;
c) Brief description of the incident, including an indication of the category of the
cause and of the effects produced, in accordance with the taxonomy defined by the
CNCS, whenever possible, the respective detail;
d) Possible estimate of the impact, considering:
i) Number of users affected by the service disruption;
ii) Duration of the incident;
iii) Geographical distribution, as regards the area affected by the incident,
including an indication of the cross-border impact;
iv) Other information that the essential and important entity considers relevant.
3 — Where necessary, the essential, important or relevant public entity sends to
the competent cybersecurity authority an update of the initial notification within 72
hours after the finding of the significant incident, revising the information referred
to in the preceding paragraph and providing an initial assessment of the significant
incident, including its severity and impact, as well as, where available, the
indicators of exposure to risks.
Article 43
Notification of the end of the significant impact
1 — The notification of the end of the significant impact of the incident must be
submitted to the competent cybersecurity authority, without undue delay and
within 24 hours after the end of the impact.
2 — The notification of the end of the significant impact must include at least the
following information:
a) Update of the information transmitted in the initial notification, if any;
b) Brief description of the measures adopted for the resolution of the incident;
c) Description of the impact situation existing at the time the significant impact
ceased, namely:
i) Number of users affected by the service disruption;
ii) Duration of the incident;
iii) Geographical distribution, as regards the area affected by the incident,
including an indication of cross-border impact;
iv) Estimated time for the full recovery of services.
Article 44
Final and intermediate reports
1 — The final report must be submitted to the competent cybersecurity authority
within 30 working days from the date of the notification of the end of the
significant impact of the incident.
2 — The final report must include the following information:
a) Date and time at which the incident took on significant impact;
b) Date and time at which the incident lost significant impact;
c) Impact of the incident, considering:
i) Number of users affected by the service disruption;
ii) Duration of the incident;
iii) Geographical distribution, as regards the area affected by the incident,
including an indication of cross-border impact;
iv) Description of the incident, with an indication of the category of the cause and
of the effects produced, in accordance with the taxonomy defined by the CNCS, and
the respective detail;
d) Indication of the measures adopted to mitigate the incident;
e) Description of the residual impact situation existing at the date of the final
notification, namely:
i) Number of users affected by the service disruption;
ii) Geographical distribution, as regards the area affected by the incident, including
an indication of cross-border impact;
iii) Estimated time for the full recovery of the services still affected;
iv) Indication, whenever applicable, of the submission of a notification of the
incident concerned to the competent authorities, namely to the Public Prosecution
Service or to the CNPD and to other sectoral authorities, under the terms provided
for in the applicable legal and regulatory provisions;
v) Other information that the essential and important entity considers relevant.
3 — In the event that, once the time limit for submitting the final report has
elapsed, the incident is still ongoing, the essential, important or relevant public
entity concerned must submit an intermediate report to the competent
cybersecurity authority, at the request of these entities and on a weekly basis until
the time of submission of the final report.
4 — The intermediate report must include the following information:
a) Update of the information transmitted in the initial notification, if any;
b) Brief description of the measures adopted for the resolution of the incident;
c) Description of the impact situation existing at the time the significant impact
ceased, namely:
i) Number of users affected by the service disruption;
ii) Duration of the incident;
iii) Geographical distribution, as regards the area affected by the incident,
including an indication of cross-border impact;
iv) Estimated time for the full recovery of services.
Article 45
Voluntary notifications of relevant information
1 — Without prejudice to the obligation to notify incidents provided for in this
Decree-Law, any natural or legal person may notify, on a voluntary basis, the
occurrence of incidents, cyber threats, near misses or vulnerabilities.
2 — Voluntary notifications do not give rise to additional obligations for the
notifying entity.
3 — The provisions of Articles 42 to 44 apply, with the necessary adaptations, to
voluntary notifications, without prejudice to the priority to be given to the handling
of mandatory notifications.
Article 46
Requests for information
The competent cybersecurity authority may request from essential, important or
relevant public entities the relevant information or determine the necessary
actions, under the legally applicable terms, when it becomes aware, by any means,
of a potential incident, the provisions of Articles 42 to 44 applying with the
necessary adaptations.
Article 47
Protection of information
1 — The sending of information by the CNCS or, where applicable, by the national
sectoral cybersecurity authorities, under this Decree-Law, to national, European
Union or other Member State competent authorities or entities is limited to what is
necessary and proportionate, in accordance with the legislation applicable in
matters of personal data protection, namely the GDPR, Law No. 26/2016, of 22
August, as currently worded, Law No. 58/2019, of 8 August, and Law No. 59/2019,
of 8 August.
2 — The competent cybersecurity authority guarantees the adequate protection of
the information and data, whatever their nature, transmitted by essential,
important and relevant public entities as regards confidentiality, business secrecy
and professional secrecy.
3 — Paragraph 2 applies, with the necessary adaptations, to the information
provided by natural and legal persons who make a notification under the preceding
article.
Article 48
Communication to the recipients of services
1 — Essential, important and relevant public entities communicate to the recipients
of their services, without undue delay, any incidents with a significant impact that
are likely to adversely affect them.
2 — Essential, important and relevant public entities communicate to the recipients
of their services potentially affected by a significant cyber threat, without undue
delay, the measures or remedies that those recipients can take in response to the
threat and, where appropriate, communicate to them the cyber threat concerned.
3 — The communication referred to in the preceding paragraph does not exempt
the entities concerned from the duty to adopt, at their own expense, the
appropriate and immediate measures to prevent or remedy any threats and to
restore the normal level of security of the service they provide.
4 — The information referred to in the preceding paragraphs must be provided free
of charge and in easily comprehensible language.
SECTION III
Communication of incidents, information to the public and response
Article 49
Communication between authorities
1 — The national sectoral and special cybersecurity authorities communicate to the
CNCS all incidents of which they are notified under the provisions of Article 40,
and inform that authority of their respective evolution.
2 — For the purposes of Article 21, the CNCS communicates to the Secretary-
General of the Internal Security System, without undue delay, the incidents of
which they are notified under the provisions of Article 40 which are liable to be
qualified as large-scale.
3 — The CNCS informs, when it considers it necessary, the national sectoral and
special cybersecurity authorities of voluntary notifications under Article 45
4 — The provisions of this article apply, with the necessary adaptations, to
notifications made under Article 42
5 — The communications referred to in the preceding paragraphs are made
immediately, by electronic means.
Article 50
Communication to entities within the European Union or its Member States
1 — Whenever justified, namely when a significant incident involves at least one
other Member State of the European Union, the CNCS must inform the other
affected Member States, designated under Article 8 of Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of
the European Parliament and of the Council, of 14 December 2022, and ENISA of
its occurrence, with the involvement of the cooperation channels in matters of
police cooperation and in matters of intelligence services.
2 — The communication referred to in the preceding paragraph includes the
information received through the notifications made under Articles 42 et seq.
3 — It is incumbent on the CNCS, in its capacity as single point of contact, to
submit quarterly to ENISA a summary report including anonymised and
aggregated data on the significant incidents, incidents, cyber threats and near
misses notified under Articles 40 and 45
Article 51
Information to the public
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority must inform the public of the
occurrence of a significant incident, after consulting the entity concerned, when:
a) It is necessary to inform the public in order to prevent the incident or to deal
with an ongoing incident;
b) The disclosure of the significant incident is in the public interest.
2 — The competent cybersecurity authority must also require the entity concerned
to disclose the significant incident to the public, when the situations referred to in
the preceding paragraph are at issue.
3 — The competent cybersecurity authority must inform the public of a significant
incident, upon a request from a competent authority of another Member State of
the European Union.
4 — The communication to the public provided for in this article is without
prejudice to cooperation in the context of ongoing criminal investigations, or of
those covered by the regimes of judicial secrecy and State secrecy.
Article 52
Response to notifications
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority responds to the notifying entity without
undue delay and, where possible, within 24 hours after receipt of the initial
notification provided for in Article 42
2 — The competent cybersecurity authority provides in its response, namely, its
initial feedback on the significant incident and, at the request of the entity,
guidance or operational advice on the implementation of possible mitigation
measures.
3 — In situations of serious and proven risk from the impact of the incident notified
under Article 40, the competent cybersecurity authority may impose, as an
immediate enforcement measure, the interruption of the provision of the service on
the essential, important or relevant public entity concerned, or the cessation of
conduct that infringes this Decree-Law, if the entity does not do so voluntarily.
4 — In cases of well-founded suspicion of the criminal nature of the significant
incident, the competent cybersecurity authority must also provide guidance on the
notification of the significant incident to the police authorities.
5 — The provisions of the preceding paragraphs apply, with the necessary
adaptations, to incidents, near misses or cyber threats that have been notified on a
voluntary basis under Article 45
CHAPTER VI
Supervision and enforcement
SECTION I
Supervisory and enforcement measures
Article 53
Principles
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority, in its capacity as supervisory and
enforcement authority, monitors and supervises compliance with this Decree-Law
and adopts the measures necessary to ensure such compliance.
2 — Supervisory and enforcement activities are guided, namely, by the principles of
pursuit of the public interest, legality, efficiency, effectiveness and proportionality,
and must minimise, whenever possible, their impact on the public, social and
business activities of the supervised entities.
3 — The supervisory activity is based on risk-assessment methodologies and, on the
basis of that assessment and of the principles referred to in the preceding
paragraph, may determine the priority allocation of resources and the measures to
be adopted according to the risk matrix applicable to the entity concerned, namely
as regards the conduct, frequency or type of on-site inspections, targeted security
audits or security scans and the type of information to be requested.
4 — Supervisory and enforcement activities are exercised with operational
autonomy, including those directed at relevant public entities.
5 — Supervisory and enforcement activities respect the guarantees of private
persons legally and constitutionally provided for.
Article 54
Supervisory measures relating to essential entities
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority has, in relation to essential entities,
powers to subject them to the following measures:
a) On-site inspections and remote supervision, including random checks conducted
by qualified professionals;
b) Regular or targeted security audits, carried out by the competent authority itself
or, where justified, by an entity duly qualified for that purpose offering guarantees
of independence;
c) Ad hoc audits, namely on the grounds of the finding of a significant incident,
non-compliance with orders, instructions and guidelines of the competent
cybersecurity authority or infringement of this Decree-Law by the entity
concerned;
d) Security scans based on objective, non-discriminatory, fair and transparent risk-
assessment criteria, if necessary in cooperation with the entity concerned;
e) Requests for information necessary to assess compliance with the cybersecurity
measures referred to in Articles 27 et seq. adopted by the entity concerned;
f) Requests for access to data, documents and information necessary for the
performance of its supervisory tasks;
g) Requests for the submission of evidence of the implementation of cybersecurity
policies and procedures.
2 — The targeted audits referred to in point (b) of the preceding paragraph are
based on the risk analysis carried out by the competent cybersecurity authority, on
the risk analysis carried out by the audited entity or on other available risk-related
information, namely that contained in the technical harmonisation instructions and
the risk matrices drawn up by the CNCS, under paragraph 4 of Article 26, as well
as in the orders, instructions and guidelines of the competent cybersecurity
authority.
3 — The costs of the targeted audits referred to in points (b) of paragraph 1 are
borne by the audited entity, unless otherwise decided, with reasons, by the
competent cybersecurity authority.
4 — The requests for information and evidence referred to in points (e) to (g) of
paragraph 1 must indicate their purpose, specify the information requested and set
an appropriate and reasonable time limit for the essential entity to respond to
them.
Article 55
Supervisory measures relating to important and relevant public entities
1 — Whenever it obtains evidence, indications or information that an important or
relevant public entity is not complying with this Decree-Law, the competent
cybersecurity authority applies the ex post supervisory measures provided for in
the following paragraphs.
2 — The competent cybersecurity authority has, in relation to important entities,
powers to subject them to the following measures:
a) On-site inspections and remote ex post supervision conducted by qualified
professionals;
b) Targeted security audits carried out by the competent authority itself or, where
justified, by an entity duly qualified for that purpose offering guarantees of
independence;
c) Ad hoc audits, namely on the grounds of the finding of a significant incident,
non-compliance with orders, instructions and guidelines of the competent
cybersecurity authority or infringement of this Decree-Law by the entity
concerned;
d) Security scans based on objective, non-discriminatory, fair and transparent risk-
assessment criteria, if necessary in cooperation with the entity concerned;
e) Requests for information necessary to assess compliance with the cybersecurity
measures referred to in Articles 27 et seq. adopted by the entity concerned;
f) Requests for access to data, documents and any information necessary for the
performance of its supervisory tasks;
g) Requests for the submission of evidence of the implementation of cybersecurity
policies and procedures, including those inherent in obtaining the certificates
referred to in paragraph 1 of Article 34
3 — The targeted audits referred to in point (b) of the preceding paragraph are
based on the risk analysis carried out by the competent cybersecurity authority, on
the risk analysis carried out by the audited entity or on other available risk-related
information, namely that contained in the technical harmonisation instructions and
the risk matrices drawn up by the CNCS, under paragraph 4 of Article 26, as well
as in the orders, instructions and guidelines of the competent cybersecurity
authority.
4 — The costs of the targeted audits referred to in point (b) of paragraph 2 are
borne by the audited entity, unless otherwise decided, with reasons, by the
competent cybersecurity authority.
5 — The requests for information and evidence referred to in points (e) to (g) of
paragraph 2 must indicate their purpose, specify the information requested and set
an appropriate and reasonable time limit for the essential entity to respond to
them.
Article 56
Enforcement measures
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority may, in relation to essential, important
and relevant public entities, adopt measures including the following:
a) Warnings about infringements of the duties arising from this Decree-Law and
from the respective applicable regulatory regime;
b) Binding orders or instructions with a view to the adoption of the measures
necessary to prevent, stop or correct an incident, setting the time limits for their
implementation and the respective reporting;
c) Binding orders or instructions with a view to the correction of deficiencies or
infringements of this Decree-Law;
d) Binding orders or instructions with a view to compliance with the provisions of
Article 26 et seq. or, in the case of a relevant public entity, with the provisions of
Article 33, or also with a view to compliance with the provisions of Articles 40 et
seq.;
e) Orders requiring the entities concerned to inform the natural or legal persons to
whom they provide services or for whom they carry out activities that are
potentially affected by a significant cyber threat of the nature of that threat, as well
as of any protective or remedial measures that may be adopted in response to that
cyber threat;
f) Orders requiring the entity concerned to implement, within a reasonable time
limit, the recommendations made as a result of a security audit;
g) Designation of a monitoring officer with well-defined tasks, for a limited period,
to oversee compliance by the entity concerned with the obligations provided for in
Articles 26 et seq., and provided for in Articles 40 et seq.;
h) Orders requiring the entity concerned to make public aspects of infringements
of this Decree-Law in a specified manner;
i) Imposition of fines under the following chapter.
2 — In the event of non-compliance, by any essential entity, with the measures
referred to in points (a) to (d) and (f) of the preceding paragraph within the time
limit set by the competent cybersecurity authority, the latter may, to the extent
strictly necessary:
a) Suspend a certification, authorisation or licence concerning part or all of the
relevant services provided or activities carried out by the entity, or order a
certification body to suspend it;
b) Request the competent body to suspend the authorisation or licence concerning
part or all of the relevant services provided or activities carried out by the entity.
3 — The temporary suspensions or prohibitions referred to in the preceding
paragraph remain in place until the entity corrects the deficiencies or complies
with the measures referred to in paragraph 1.
4 — The measures referred to in paragraph 2 do not apply to the public entities
covered by this Decree-Law, without prejudice to the exercise of powers of
direction and oversight, under the general terms.
Article 57
Blocking and redirection measures
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority may issue orders or instructions with a
view to neutralising a cyber threat, cyberattack or incident to the network and
information systems of essential, important or relevant public entities resulting
from the abusive use of domain names or IP protocol addresses, under the
following paragraphs.
2 — The types of abuse referred to in the preceding paragraph include, namely:
a) Distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks;
b) Malicious servers (Command and Control);
c) Infected equipment (communication with Command and Control);
d) Distribution of malicious code;
e) Unlawful use of third parties’ names;
f) Unsolicited electronic mail (SPAM).
3 — To the extent strictly necessary to stop the abusive use of domain names, the
competent cybersecurity authority may order, in a duly reasoned manner:
a) The TLD name registry to request the holder of a domain name registration to
adopt appropriate measures, within a set time limit, to repress a cyber threat or
respond to a cyberattack or an incident;
b) The TLD name registry or DNS service providers to block or redirect domain
names to a secure server of the CNCS, when they are manifestly dedicated to or
involved in cyberattacks or incidents and no other effective means are available to
stop the cyberattack or incident.
4 — To the extent strictly necessary to stop the abusive use of IP protocol
addresses, the CNCS may order undertakings providing electronic communications
networks and services to block or redirect a dynamic or static IP protocol address
to a secure server of the CNCS, when those addresses are manifestly dedicated to
or involved in the types of cyberattacks or incidents provided for in points (a) to (d)
of paragraph 2.
5 — The measures referred to in paragraphs 3 and 4 may not exceed a period of 60
days, which may be renewed for an equal period where there is a strong likelihood,
ascertained by means of a reasoned assessment, that the cyberattacks or incidents
originating from the same addresses will persist or resume.
6 — The provisions of this article apply equally to domain name registration service
providers.
Article 58
Procedural safeguards
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority provides an adequate statement of
reasons for its decisions applying enforcement measures, and must also, under the
general terms, hold a prior hearing of the entity concerned within a reasonable
time limit, of not less than 10 days.
2 — The prior hearing referred to in the preceding paragraph is dispensed with
whenever there is a duly reasoned need to apply immediate measures to prevent or
respond to significant incidents or cyber threats.
3 — When applying any of the enforcement measures referred to in the preceding
paragraphs, the competent cybersecurity authority must respect the procedural
safeguards of the entity, having regard to the circumstances of the specific case,
and weigh, namely:
a) The seriousness of the infringement and the importance of the provisions
infringed;
b) The duration of the infringement;
c) Any relevant previous infringements committed by the entity concerned;
d) Any material or non-material damage caused, including any financial or
economic losses, the effects on other services and the number of users affected;
e) Any measures taken by the entity to prevent or mitigate the material or non-
material damage;
f) The culpability of the agent;
g) The level of cooperation of the responsible natural or legal persons with the
competent cybersecurity authority.
4 — For the purposes of point (a) of the preceding paragraph, the following are
presumed serious:
a) Repeated violations of this Decree-Law;
b) Failure to comply with the duty to notify incidents under Articles 40 et seq.;
c) Failure to comply with the duty to remedy significant incidents;
d) Failure to comply with the duty to remedy deficiencies following binding
instructions of the competent cybersecurity authority;
e) Obstruction of audits or monitoring activities ordered by the competent
cybersecurity authority following the finding of an infringement of this Decree-Law;
f) Provision of false or grossly inaccurate information in relation to the
cybersecurity measures provided for in Articles 26 et seq., or to the notification
obligations provided for in Articles 40 et seq.
SECTION II
Cooperation between authorities with supervisory competences
Article 59
Communication of incidents and application of measures
1 — The national sectoral cybersecurity authorities and the special national
cybersecurity authorities inform the CNCS of the occurrence of significant
incidents or cyber threats, as well as of the application of supervisory and
enforcement measures in matters of cybersecurity, under the applicable regime.
2 — The application of supervisory and enforcement measures in matters of
cybersecurity, under the applicable regime, by the national sectoral cybersecurity
authorities and by the special national cybersecurity authorities is preceded by a
non-binding opinion of the CNCS, with the exception, for the national sectoral
cybersecurity authorities, of the measures provided for in point (i) of paragraph 1
of Article 56
3 — The national sectoral cybersecurity authorities and the special national
cybersecurity authorities are exempt from requesting an opinion from the CNCS
under the preceding paragraph when compliance with enforcement measures
within a period of less than 24h is at issue, without prejudice to the measures being
immediately communicated to the CNCS.
4 — The competent cybersecurity authority informs the special national
cybersecurity authorities of significant incidents that have occurred that may affect
entities in the financial sector.
5 — The transmission of the above-mentioned information must be carried out
through the platform mentioned in paragraph 7 of Article 8
Article 60
Cooperation in the field of the security of critical infrastructures
1 — Whenever the CNCS, the national sectoral cybersecurity authorities or the
special national cybersecurity authorities, as the case may be, exercise their
supervisory powers in relation to an entity referred to in paragraph 5 of Article 3,
they must inform the competent authorities resulting from the transposition of
Directive (EU) 2022/2557, of the European Parliament and the Council, of 14
December 2022.
2 — The competent authorities resulting from the transposition of Directive (EU)
2022/2557, of the European Parliament and the Council, of 14 December 2022,
may, if necessary, request that the CNCS, the national sectoral cybersecurity
authorities or the special national cybersecurity authorities, as the case may be,
exercise their supervisory powers in relation to an entity referred to in paragraph 5
of Article 3
CHAPTER VII
Sanctions regime
Article 61
Very serious administrative offences
1 — The following constitute very serious administrative offences under this
Decree-Law:
a) Failure to comply with the decisions of the member of the Government
responsible for the area of cybersecurity provided for in paragraph 3 of Article 18;
b) Failure to comply with the duty to adopt the cybersecurity measures under
Articles 27 to 29;
c) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 30;
d) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 31;
e) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 32;
f) Failure to comply with the duty to adopt the cybersecurity measures established
by the CNCS under Article 33;
g) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 34;
h) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article
36;
i) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 37;
j) Failure to comply with the duty of notification under Articles 40 to 44;
k) Failure to comply with the duty of communication under the provisions of Article
48
2 — The administrative offences referred to in the preceding paragraph are
punishable with the following fines:
a) In the case of an essential entity:
i) From € 2,000.00 to € 10,000,000.00 or 2% of the total worldwide annual
turnover, in the preceding financial year, of the essential entity concerned,
whichever is higher, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 350.00 to € 200,000.00, if committed by a natural person;
b) In the case of an important entity:
i) From € 1,250.00 to € 7,000,000.00 or in a maximum amount of not less than
1.4% of the total worldwide annual turnover, in the preceding financial year, of the
important entity concerned, whichever is higher, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 350.00 to € 200,000.00, if committed by a natural person;
c) In the case of a relevant public entity included in Group A provided for in
paragraph 2 of Article 7:
i) From € 16,000.00 to € 4,000,000.00, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 500.00 to € 16,000.00, if committed by a natural person;
d) In the case of a relevant public entity included in Group B provided for in
paragraph 3 of Article 7:
i) From € 8,000.00 to € 350,000.00, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 500.00 to € 16,000.00, if committed by a natural person.
Article 62
Serious administrative offences
1 — The following constitute serious administrative offences under this Decree-
Law:
a) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 8;
b) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 35;
c) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in paragraphs 4 and 5 of Article
36;
d) Failure to comply with the duties provided for in Article 46;
e) Failure to comply with the obligation provided for in paragraph 2 of Article 51;
f) Failure to comply with the immediate enforcement measure provided for in
paragraph 3 of Article 52;
g) Failure to comply with the warnings, binding orders or instructions given by the
competent cybersecurity authority under the provisions of points (a) to (g) of
paragraph 1 of Article 56;
h) Breach of the suspension determined under the provisions of point (a) of
paragraph 2 of Article 56;
i) Breach of the suspension determined under the provisions of point (b) of
paragraph 2 of Article 56;
j) Failure to comply with the orders or instructions provided for in Article 57
2 — The administrative offences referred to in the preceding paragraph are
punishable with the following fines:
a) In the case of an essential entity:
i) From € 1,250.00 to € 5,000,000.00 or 1% of the total worldwide annual turnover,
in the preceding financial year, of the essential entity concerned, whichever is
higher, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 250.00 to € 125,000.00, if committed by a natural person;
b) In the case of an important entity:
i) From € 875.00 to € 3,500,000.00 or in a maximum amount of not less than 0.7%
of the total worldwide annual turnover, in the preceding financial year, of the
important entity concerned, whichever is higher, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 250.00 to € 125,000.00, if committed by a natural person;
c) In the case of a relevant public entity included in “Group A” provided for in
paragraph 2 of Article 7:
i) From € 10,000.00 to € 2,500,000.00, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 375.00 to € 10,000.00, if committed by a natural person;
d) In the case of a relevant public entity included in “Group B” provided for in
paragraph 3 of Article 7:
i) From € 5,000.00 to € 225,000.00, if committed by a legal person;
ii) From € 375.00 to € 10,000.00, if committed by a natural person.
Article 63
Minor administrative offences
1 — The following are minor administrative offences:
a) The use, by entities, of an invalid, expired or revoked cybersecurity certification
mark;
b) The use of an expression or graphic element that expressly or tacitly suggests
the cybersecurity certification of a product, service or process that is not certified;
c) The wilful omission of information or the provision of false information that is
relevant to the cybersecurity certification process under way, under the terms
defined in each certification scheme;
d) Failure to comply with the requests of the Cyberspace Security Assessment
Commission, provided for in paragraph 8 of Article 18
2 — The administrative offences referred to in the preceding paragraph are
punishable with the following fines:
a) From € 875.00 to € 45,000.00, if committed by a legal person;
b) From € 250.00 to € 3,750.00, if committed by a natural person.
Article 64
Negligence
The administrative offences referred to in paragraph 1 of Article 61, in paragraph 1
of Article 62 and in points (a) and (b) of paragraph 1 of Article 63 are likewise
punishable when committed negligently, the minimum and maximum limits of the
fines being reduced by half.
Article 65
Exemption from the application of fines
All essential, important and relevant public entities may, by means of a duly
reasoned request, ask the competent cybersecurity authority for exemption from
the application of the fines referred to in paragraph 2 of Article 61 and in
paragraph 2 of Article 62, on the grounds of the absence of an internal procedure
for the adaptation of those entities to the new legal regime, during 12 months from
the entry into force of this Decree-Law.
Article 66
Determination of the amount of the fine
1 — The specific fine is determined according to the seriousness of the specific
unlawfulness of the act, the culpability of the agent, the agent’s economic situation
and the economic benefit that the agent derived from the commission of the
administrative offence.
2 — In determining the specific unlawfulness of the act and the culpability of the
agent, regard is had to the following circumstances:
a) The seriousness of the infringement;
b) The duration of the infringement;
c) The occasional or repeated nature of the infringement;
d) The damage caused, including any financial or economic losses, the effects on
other services and the number of users affected;
e) The measures taken by the entity to prevent or mitigate the damage referred to
in the preceding point;
f) The level of cooperation of the responsible natural or legal persons with the
competent cybersecurity authority.
3 — For the purposes of point (a) of the preceding paragraph, the following are
presumed serious:
a) Repeated violations of this Decree-Law;
b) Failure to notify incidents under Articles 40 et seq.;
c) Failure to remedy significant incidents;
d) Failure to remedy deficiencies following binding instructions of the competent
authorities;
e) Obstruction of audits or monitoring activities ordered by the competent
cybersecurity authority following the finding of an infringement of this Decree-Law;
f) The provision of false or grossly inaccurate information in relation to the
cybersecurity measures and duties relating to the cybersecurity measures, under
the provisions of Articles 27 et seq., or to the notification obligations, under the
provisions of Articles 40 et seq.
4 — The provisions of point (f) of the preceding paragraph are without prejudice to
liability under the Penal Code.
5 — Except in the case of intent, the institution of administrative offence
proceedings depends on a prior warning to the agent, by the competent
cybersecurity authority, to comply with the omitted obligation or to restore
compliance with the prohibition breached within a reasonable time limit.
Article 67
Ancillary sanctions and other determinations
Where the seriousness of the infringement and the culpability of the offender so
justify, the competent cybersecurity authority may determine, simultaneously with
the fine:
a) The publication in the Diário da República and in one of the newspapers with the
largest national, regional or local circulation, depending on the relevant
geographical market, at the expense of the offender, of an extract of the conviction
decision or, at least, of the operative part of the conviction decision handed down in
proceedings instituted under this Decree-Law, after it has become final;
b) A prohibition on participating in public procurement procedures, where
applicable;
c) The adoption and implementation of a cybersecurity training plan, to be
implemented within six months;
d) The adoption or amendment of a security plan, to be implemented within six
months;
e) The suspension of the provision of the service until the omitted duties are
complied with;
f) The temporary prohibition of the holders of the management, direction and
administration bodies from exercising their respective functions.
Article 68
Periodic penalty payments
1 — The addressees of a decision of the competent cybersecurity authority become
liable to pay a pecuniary sum for each day of delay in compliance, counted from the
date of the respective notification.
2 — For the purposes of the preceding paragraph, a periodic penalty payment is
the imposition on the agent of the payment of a pecuniary sum for each day of non-
compliance occurring beyond the time limit set for compliance with the obligation.
3 — The periodic penalty payment is set according to criteria of reasonableness
and proportionality, the daily amount of the penalty provided for in the preceding
paragraph being set at € 500.00 when committed by a legal person and at € 100.00
when committed by a natural person.
4 — The daily amounts set may increase for each day of non-compliance, and may
not, in any case, exceed the maximum duration of 30 days.
Article 69
Limitation period for proceedings
1 — Proceedings for serious and very serious administrative offences are
extinguished by limitation once a period of five years has elapsed since the
commission of the administrative offence, without prejudice to the causes of
interruption and suspension provided for under the general terms.
2 — Proceedings for minor administrative offences are extinguished by limitation
once a period of three years has elapsed since the commission of the administrative
offence, without prejudice to the causes of interruption and suspension provided
for under the general terms.
Article 70
Limitation period for fines and ancillary sanctions
1 — The limitation period for fines and ancillary sanctions is:
a) Three years, in the case of serious and very serious administrative offences;
b) Two years, in the case of minor administrative offences.
2 — The period is counted from the date on which the conviction decision becomes
definitive or final.
Article 71
Rule on the competence of the competent authorities
The institution and investigation of administrative offence proceedings, as well as
the application of fines, fall within the competence of the competent cybersecurity
authority.
Article 72
Notifications
1 — Notifications made by the competent cybersecurity authorities are made
electronically or, at the reasoned request of the entity, by registered letter or in
person.
2 — Electronic notification is made by making it available in a digital area with
access reserved to the addressee, integrated in the platform provided for in
paragraph 7 of Article 8 and associated with the email address registered on it by
the addressee, and also, cumulatively, through the public electronic notification
service (SPNE), whenever it is found that the addressee has joined it, under
Decree-Law No. 93/2017, of 1 August, as currently worded.
3 — The making available is accompanied by the sending of a notice to the
addressee to the email address registered on the platform provided for in
paragraph 7 of Article 8, indicating the sending authority and the means of access
to the addressee’s reserved area.
4 — Electronic notification is deemed to have been made on the date of electronic
consultation of the digital area with reserved access of the platform provided for in
paragraph 7 of Article 8 or, if this does not occur within the first three days from
receipt, at the end of that period.
5 — Postal notification is presumed to have been made on the third working day
after registration.
Article 73
Proceeds of fines
The proceeds of fines revert:
a) 60% to the State;
b) 40% to the CNCS or to the competent national sectoral cybersecurity authority,
depending on the entity that instituted and investigated the proceedings.
Article 74
Costs
1 — Costs relating to the charges for processing, archiving and making available
the administrative offence proceedings are also due for such proceedings.
2 — The decisions of the competent cybersecurity authority on the substance of the
proceedings must set the amount of the costs.
3 — The costs are intended to cover the expenses incurred in the proceedings.
4 — Reimbursement of the expenses with notifications and communications,
audiovisual means and materials used in the proceedings is calculated:
a) Where the proceedings are processed, totally or partially, on paper, at the rate of
half of 0.5 UC for the first 50 sheets or fraction of the file and one tenth of a UC for
each subsequent set of 25 sheets or fraction of the file, without prejudice to the
provisions of the following paragraphs;
b) Where the proceedings are processed primarily in digital form, up to a maximum
of 5 UC, having regard to the complexity of the proceedings and the acts
performed.
5 — The costs also include the following charges:
a) The remuneration of experts, translators, interpreters and technical consultants;
b) The payment due for travel or payments to any entity for the cost of technical
services, certificates or other elements of information and evidence.
6 — Where copies or certificates of the proceedings or of parts thereof are
provided, in physical or digital form, at the request of the defendant, an amount
calculated under the terms provided for in the same paragraphs is added to the
amount referred to in the preceding paragraphs.
7 — The costs are borne by the defendant and co-liable parties under this Decree-
Law, in the event of the application of an admonition, a fine or an ancillary
sanction.
8 — The costs revert to the CNCS or to the national sectoral cybersecurity
authority, depending on the competence for processing the administrative offence
proceedings.
Article 75
Compliance with an omitted duty
Whenever the administrative offence results from the omission of a duty, the
application of the sanction and the payment of the fine do not exempt the offender
from complying with it, if compliance is still possible.
Article 76
Suspension of the enforcement of the fine
1 — The competent cybersecurity authority suspends the enforcement of the fine
imposed, having regard to the non-repeated nature of the agent’s unlawful
conduct, to the circumstances of the commission of the infringement and to the
agent’s conduct before and after the crime, whenever it concludes that the simple
censure of the act, the subjection to ancillary sanctions and the threat of a fine
adequately and sufficiently achieve the preventive and corrective purposes of the
sanction.
2 — The competent cybersecurity authority, if it deems it appropriate for achieving
the purposes of the punishment, makes the suspension of the enforcement of the
fine subject to compliance with the sanctions and determinations provided for in
Article 67, or with other duties it considers relevant.
3 — The conviction decision always specifies the grounds for the suspension and its
conditions, including the respective duration.
4 — The period of suspension is set between one and three years from the
notification of the conviction decision or of the final judicial decision ruling on it.
Article 77
Revocation of the suspension of the enforcement of the fine
1 — If, during the period of suspension, the convicted party fails to comply with any
of the sanctions or determinations provided for in Article 67 or commits a very
serious or serious administrative offence, the competent cybersecurity authority,
after due procedure, revokes the decision suspending the enforcement of the fine.
2 — Revocation determines the duty of immediate payment of the fine, without the
defendant being able to demand reimbursement of any payments made or expenses
incurred during the prior compliance with the ancillary sanctions imposed on the
defendant.
Article 78
Extinction of the fine
The fine is declared extinguished if, once the period of its suspension has elapsed,
there are no grounds that could lead to its revocation.
Article 79
Personal data breach
1 — Whenever the competent cybersecurity authority obtains a reasonable degree
of certainty, in the course of a supervisory action or of the imposition of an
enforcement measure, that the infringement of the obligations established in
Articles 27 to 29 and in Articles 40 to 43 by an essential or important entity may
entail a personal data breach, within the meaning of point 12 of Article 4 of the
GDPR, which must be notified under Article 33 of that GDPR, that authority must,
without undue delay, inform the CNPD.
2 — Where the CNPD imposes a fine, under point (i) of paragraph 2 of Article 58 of
the GDPR and the other applicable national law, the competent cybersecurity
authority is prevented from imposing a fine as a result of the commission of the
same infringement under this Decree-Law, without prejudice to the provisions of
the following paragraph.
3 — The competent cybersecurity authority may impose the enforcement measures
provided for in points (a) to (h) of paragraph 1 of Article 56 on essential and
important entities whose breach of the obligations arising from this Decree-Law
results in a personal data breach incident.
Article 80
Challenge of the decisions of the competent cybersecurity authority
1 — Without prejudice to the provisions of paragraph 3, once the decision handed
down by the competent cybersecurity authority in administrative offence
proceedings has been challenged, that authority forwards the respective case file
to the Public Prosecution Service, preferably electronically, within 20 working
days, and may attach submissions, as well as other elements or information it
considers relevant for the decision of the case, and also offer means of evidence.
2 — The forwarding of the case file electronically dispenses with the sending of the
respective originals, without prejudice to the duty to produce the procedural
documents on paper and the originals of the documents contained therein, where
they exist, whenever the Public Prosecution Service or the judge so determines.
3 — The decisions or any measures adopted and applied by the competent
cybersecurity authority in administrative offence proceedings may be challenged
before the courts of law, the appeal having to be lodged with the competent
cybersecurity authority.
4 — The challenge of any decisions handed down by the competent cybersecurity
authority which, in administrative offence proceedings, determine the application
of fines, ancillary sanctions or periodic penalty payments has suspensive effect.
5 — The challenge of the other decisions or measures of the competent
cybersecurity authority adopted in administrative offence proceedings has merely
devolutive effect and complies with the rules provided for in this article.
6 — The competent cybersecurity authority, the Public Prosecution Service and the
defendants may object to the court deciding by order, without a trial hearing.
7 — The competent cybersecurity authority has standing to appeal autonomously
against any judgments and orders that are not merely procedural, including those
concerning nullities and other preliminary or incidental questions, or the
application of precautionary measures, as well as to respond to appeals lodged.
8 — The decisions of the courts of law that admit appeal, under the terms provided
for in the regime of administrative offences (ilícito de mera ordenação social),
approved by Decree-Law No. 433/82, of 27 October, as currently worded, may be
challenged before the Lisbon Court of Appeal.
9 — The Court of Appeal, within the scope of the competence provided for in the
preceding paragraph, decides as the court of last instance, no ordinary appeal lying
against its judgments.
Article 81
Subsidiary law
In matters of administrative offences, in all matters not provided for in this Decree-
Law, the provisions of the regime of administrative offences (ilícito de mera
ordenação social), approved by Decree-Law No. 433/82, of 27 October, as currently
worded, apply subsidiarily.
CHAPTER VIII
Complementary provisions
SECTION I
Other provisions
Article 82
Supervisory fee
1 — A supervisory fee may be charged to essential and important entities in return
for the supervisory acts performed, to be set according to the costs necessary for
the provision of supervisory services.
2 — Supervisory fees comply with the principle of proportionality and are set in
accordance with objective and transparent criteria.
3 — The regime governing the fees referred to in the preceding paragraphs is set
by ministerial order (portaria) of the members of the Government responsible for
the areas of finance and cybersecurity.
Article 83
Communications
1 — Communications between entities and the CNCS, or the national sectoral
cybersecurity authorities referred to in point (a) of paragraph 2 of Article 15,
including incident notifications under Articles 40 et seq., must follow the format
and procedure defined by the CNCS in a regulation to be approved by the CNCS.
2 — In the absence of an applicable regulatory provision, all communications
addressed to the competent cybersecurity authority, within the scope of this
Decree-Law, as well as the sending of information, must be carried out by
electronic means.
3 — In cases where the entity temporarily lacks the operational capacity to ensure
the communication provided for in the preceding paragraphs, or in cases where the
website of the competent cybersecurity authority is unavailable, as a result of the
incident or for another duly justified reason of an eminently technical nature, the
notification may be made, on an exceptional basis, by email or by telephone.
4 — The format and procedure referred to in paragraph 1 are adopted by the
CNCS, after prior hearing of the competent national sectoral cybersecurity
authorities, which may also adopt their own formats and procedures, adapted to
their specificities, as referred to in paragraph 1.
5 — The cases referred to in paragraph 3 are the subject of technical instructions
of the CNCS, adopted in coordination with the national sectoral cybersecurity
authorities.
Article 84
Security and integrity of information
1 — The CNCS and the national sectoral cybersecurity authorities competent under
the provisions of point (a) of paragraph 2 of Article 15 keep and manage the
information on security and integrity in a secure information system, in accordance
with the provisions concerning the security of classified information at national
level and within the international organisations to which Portugal is a party.
2 — Access to the electronic systems and websites for the processing of the
notifications provided for in this Decree-Law should preferably be made using an
electronic identification system with a high level of assurance, under the terms
defined by Articles 8 and 9 of Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, of the European
Parliament and of the Council, of 23 July 2014, on electronic identification and
trust services, namely by means of the Citizen Card and the Digital Mobile Key, as
amended by Directive (EU) 2022/2555, of the European Parliament and of the
Council, of 14 December 2022, and by Regulation (EU) 2024/1183, of the European
Parliament and of the Council, of 11 April 2024.
CHAPTER IX
Final provisions
Article 85
Approval of the national plan for response to large-scale cybersecurity
crises and incidents
The plan referred to in Article 13 is approved within six months after the entry into
force of this Decree-Law.
Article 86
Provision of resources and operational independence of the CNCS
In order to pursue its attributions and exercise the competences provided for in
this Decree-Law, the CNCS must be provided with the necessary resources and
benefits from operational independence in relation to the supervised entities.
Article 87
Interoperability and access to information
1 — The CNCS has free access to the national databases and registers relevant for
the fulfilment of the attributions and exercise of the competences provided for in
this Decree-Law and other legislation in matters of cybersecurity, in particular for
the assignment or confirmation of the qualification of entities.
2 — The public entities responsible for the national databases and registers
provided for in the preceding paragraph provide access to them by means of an
interoperability solution stipulated in a protocol and suitable for that purpose.
3 — The failure to sign the protocols referred to in the preceding paragraph does
not prevent access to the relevant information by the CNCS, and the public entities
responsible for the national databases and registers must provide all the necessary
information whenever requested by the CNCS.

Article 1
Enterprise
An enterprise is considered to be any entity which, irrespective of its legal form,
carries out an economic activity. In particular, entities carrying out a craft activity
or other activities on an individual or family basis, partnerships or associations
regularly engaged in an economic activity are considered as such.
Article 2
Categories
1 — The category of micro, small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) is made up
of enterprises which employ fewer than 250 persons and which have an annual
turnover not exceeding 50 million euros, or an annual balance sheet total not
exceeding 43 million euros.
2 — Within the SME category, a small enterprise is defined as an enterprise which
employs fewer than 50 persons and whose annual turnover or annual balance sheet
total does not exceed 10 million euros.
3 — Within the SME category, a microenterprise is defined as an enterprise which
employs fewer than 10 persons and whose annual turnover or annual balance sheet
total does not exceed 2 million euros.
